# More Words Than Reality — source-example revision proposal

Status: full proposed script and art plan awaiting root review before voice. Five changed sentences preserve fifteen recordings.

Spine: A convincing research package is not yet a verified contribution; the book's illustrative retrieval project mistakes a reported gain for an established causal mechanism, until a rival context-length explanation demands a different test and a human decision about research priorities.

Reading gain: Learn how to separate an observed improvement from its proposed explanation, identify shared evidence dependencies and choose a check that could change a specific claim.

## Complete proposed speech

### Scene 1
1. More Words Than Reality examines research that looks mature before independent checks justify it.
2. The problem extends beyond fabricated facts to convincing architectures, tests, and validation packages.
3. The book separates perceived achievement from what survives comparison, execution, and attempted falsification.
4. It proposes tools for checking knowledge claims while preserving explicit human responsibility for value judgments.
5. Readers gain a framework for challenging apparent progress without dismissing useful AI-assisted research.

### Scene 2
1. One illustrative project claims better answers come from preserving causal relationships.
2. Its three experiments share a dataset split, corpus, evaluator, and implementation.
3. A rival explanation is simpler: the method receives more usable context.
4. The book proposes equalizing context and testing an independently curated dataset.
5. That comparison could challenge the mechanism claim instead of merely extending the demonstration.

### Scene 3
1. A proposed research system would record whether a statement is assumed, tested, or demonstrated.
2. Source checks, execution, baselines, and counterexamples would challenge different aspects of the claim.
3. The book also separates evidential assessment from decisions about what is worth pursuing.
4. These guardian architectures are research proposals, not established machines for certifying discovery.
5. Their own evaluation must resist the circular validation they are designed to expose.

### Scene 4
1. Begin with the taxonomy of illusions and its concrete tests of apparent progress.
2. Keep published evidence, the author's synthesis, and new proposals distinct as you read.
3. The institutional chapters explain why tools alone cannot repair incentives that reward appearance.
4. More Words Than Reality turns frustration with plausible research into an inspectable research programme.
5. Read it to ask what would survive if the convincing presentation were removed.

## Original art plan

Art identity: editorial research proof sheets, saturated aubergine claims, cobalt execution records, copper evidence dependencies and verdigris unresolved alternatives. Vary view from a large persuasive claim sheet to an experiment comparison at matching scale, a claim-state work surface, then a human choice over the next experiment. Do not copy prior book terminals or a desk of generic named modules.

1. A large research proof sheet says a method preserves causal relationships. Executed-code/test artifacts accumulate around that exact claim, then separate from it: running code is visible evidence for execution, not automatic evidence of the explanation. This brings the opening's apparent-maturity problem into a particular research object.
2. The AppendixE example becomes a comparison: method M vs baseline B, three reported gains with a visible shared D1/corpus/evaluator/implementation lineage. The alternative 'more context' changes the proposed test. Two context holders are adjusted to the same capacity; an independent D2 enters the next-test setup. No result of that future test is displayed. Use symbolic identifiers exactly as the source and no fabricated performance bars or numerical scores.
3. Keep the same mechanism claim and its evidence references visible while statuses remain distinct: reported improvement, mechanism unconfirmed, next discriminating test. Proposed research tooling is represented as inspection and recorded revision obligations, never a certification machine or autonomous judge.
4. Two legitimate next research actions differ: another breadth test versus a context-controlled mechanism test. A researcher retains the choice, with mechanism understanding an explicit project priority rather than a universal best objective. The book returns through its taxonomy, independence checks and institutional chapters. No outcome or winner of the experiments is invented.

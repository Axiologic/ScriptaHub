When the office outlives the person
Egregopathy asks how ordinary participants can sustain an institution that behaves without remorse.
Its proposed concept concerns organizational patterns, rather than a psychiatric diagnosis of groups.
The book traces boundaries that separate harmful consequences from authority and moral language.
Its distinctive challenge is that replacing a villain may leave the mechanism intact.
The argument also turns its own persuasive method into an object of criticism.

The consequence stays outside
An office can change occupants while preserving the incentives that sustain harmful behavior.
Decision-makers receive selected information, while affected outsiders carry consequences they cannot readily contest.
Moral statements may become easier for the institution to produce than costly correction.
The book treats that pattern as a design question, not proof that every member lacks conscience.
This shifts the investigation from identifying monsters toward examining boundaries, feedback, and responsibility.

The dark lens faces its test
Historical and contemporary cases build the argument across several kinds of organized power.
The final critical turn examines whether the invented category compresses too much.
An institution has no clinical mind that can simply receive an individual psychiatric diagnosis.
The book also challenges its own selection of evidence and persuasive arrangement.
That self-examination is part of its distinctive reading experience, rather than a disposable disclaimer.

Read the mechanism and its objections
Begin with The Crime With No Criminal and follow the separation of authority from harm.
Keep the final critique in view while the historical pattern gathers force.
Readers encounter both an institutional hypothesis and an argument about how explanations acquire authority.
The useful question is which observed mechanism would survive the strongest counterexample.
Read it to examine durable harmful arrangements without confusing a memorable name with a proven diagnosis.

# Egregopathy — complete replacement proposal

Make institutional continuity visible through an explicitly imagined factory whose reporting omits downstream harm, use costly correction as the mechanism, and turn the book’s critique of its own selected evidence into a second reason to read rather than another diagnostic disclaimer.

## The objective keeps its office

1. Egregopathy asks why replacing harmful leaders can leave an institution's behavior unchanged.
2. Its proposed concept describes harm protected by roles, targets, and selective information.
3. The book treats this as institutional design, not a psychiatric diagnosis.
4. It asks whether moral concern can change objectives when correction costs something.

Art: Open close inside a factory management office: an ordinary manager signs a production target while completed goods continue along a visible delivery track. A successor enters with a different face and coat, takes the same instructions, and the track keeps moving. The retained target sheet stays attached to the actual machine control schedule, not floating as a slogan. Pull slightly wider to reveal an incomplete information route from outside the office; reserve the full downstream consequence for scene 2. A report is a work object, not a second heading. Warm ochre machinery, teal uniforms and river blue establish a lived setting. This is an authored hypothetical applying source-6, not a claim about any named company.

Emotion: Ordinary confidence meets unsettling continuity; the people remain recognizable and conscientious, not villains.

## What never reaches the report

1. Take its opening image of a company poisoning a river.
2. Imagine its managers seeing revenue reports while a downstream household loses usable water.
3. Different departments defend production, legal compliance, and reputation through separate measures.
4. An apology changes the message, while the discharge continues unchanged.
5. The book's test is whether evidence of harm can constrain that process.

Art: A panoramic cutaway connects the same factory's discharge pipe to a river beside a downstream household. An adult reaches for water, notices the visibly discolored flow and withdraws the empty container; no bodily injury or specific contaminant is depicted. At the factory, actual shipment totals reach production and revenue review, while a resident's report stops at a claims clerk whose role cannot alter the target. Three workers perform distinct tasks within the same workplace, rather than three isolated icon cards. The public apology changes on a notice board, but a moving discolored flow continues into the river. Do not turn this into proof about real departments or measured pollution. Labels identify only necessary work objects and actual authority relationships.

Emotion: Widening the frame makes the hidden cost concrete; frustration becomes a specific question about authority.

## A true example can still mislead

1. The historical chapters trace a disturbing pattern across very different forms of organized power.
2. The book later challenges the evidence it selected to make that pattern persuasive.
3. Accurate examples can mislead when contrary examples remain outside the frame.
4. Institutions also create public health, shared resources, scientific knowledge, and constraints on powerful actors.
5. That counterhistory tests whether the proposed concept explains more than its dramatic language suggests.

Art: Change scale and medium completely: a moving editorial crop extracts the factory harm from a larger illustrated historical spread. Every selected example remains visibly accurate, but the crop repeatedly leaves constructive collective work outside. Then the spread unfolds to reveal a staffed clinic, people maintaining a common irrigation channel, and a researcher consulting preserved observations; develop these as small inhabited environments rather than symbol rows. An editor moves the crop to compare the missing context with the chosen case. No fabricated counts or claim that the constructive cases cancel harm. Use plum binding, warm coral editorial marks and vivid civic scenes. This adapts the final chapter's selection critique; it is a nonfiction argument, not a protected fiction twist.

Emotion: The confident pattern becomes uncomfortable self-examination, then a more generous view of collective action.

## Concern needs authority

1. Readers gain two investigations: how institutions resist correction, and how arguments recruit belief.
2. The Moral Turing Test asks what an institution's apology makes impossible afterward.
3. Imagine authority pausing that harmful production, accepting a cost rather than changing only its language.
4. The Book as a Small Egregopath then questions the argument's own selective framing.
5. Read it to examine that mechanism while testing the boundaries of the explanation itself.

Art: Return to a close physical process rather than the whole office: in an explicitly imagined test, the same resident evidence now reaches a decision-maker with authority over the production control. The decision-maker pauses the harmful operation; the shipment track stops with an uncompleted order still visible. This enacts a cost-bearing constraint, not an empirical success story, permanent cleanup, universal reform guarantee or operational factory procedure. Existing discoloration in the river does not instantly disappear. End with the book opened to its two real chapter headings and a small continuation of the paused mechanism, avoiding duplicate large headings. Preserve the whole subtitle lane and keep people/labels clear during evidence travel. No invented fines, compensation amounts, contaminant data or diagnosis.

Emotion: Conditional agency replaces fatalism; end curious about the book's two-sided investigation rather than morally triumphant.

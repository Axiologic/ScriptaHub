# Investing in an AI-Dominated Economy — revised introduction

Complete proposed script and original art direction; public film unchanged.

## Useful can still be fragile

1. A useful AI product can succeed with customers and still lose its independence.
2. Investing in an AI-Dominated Economy builds its thesis around that uncomfortable distinction.
3. The book presents the strategic doctrine behind Outfinity Venture Studio.
4. It asks what a company retains when competitors can rent the same intelligence.
5. Readers gain a framework for examining that wager, including reasons it could fail.

Visual action: A buyer receives a useful review report from an independent software team. An existing enterprise suite offers the same visible report function. Keep the buyer and report constant: usefulness persists while the separate supplier faces a retention question. No named company or measured outcome.

## What survives the feature?

1. Imagine a review tool whose main attraction is generating a polished report.
2. An enterprise suite could bundle that feature into a service customers already use.
3. The book distinguishes demand for the feature from bargaining power for its supplier.
4. It asks what each deployment accumulates beyond another sale.
5. Reusable evaluations and lawful learning rights matter only when they actually strengthen the company.

Visual action: Close view of the same report inside two procurement options. ## A different social promise

1. The social chapters ask what makes repeated cooperation credible beyond an attention feed.
2. Consider a small group with shared work, memory, and rules its members understand.
3. The book proposes shared brands that independent communities and ventures can carry.
4. Their common identity must leave meaningful rights, local authority, and practical exit.
5. This social promise requires a different balance of power from enterprise assurance.

Visual action: Replace software screens with a small collaborative room. A participant contributes an attributed method to common work; a member leaving retains their legitimate contribution record. Community rules and brand-use conditions remain distinct. No global score, poll-as-governance guarantee, or forced belonging.

## Shared research, separate ventures

1. Outfinity proposes a shared research base supporting those two different brand promises.
2. Each venture still needs its own buyer, team, and economic logic.
3. A common component earns its place when it solves a recurring problem.
4. Confidential data and ownership rights limit what the ventures can share.
5. The book warns against forcing every company onto one convenient stack.

## Read the wager and its limits

1. This is a thesis about building institutions around AI, not selecting the next popular stock.
2. Its distinctive question is what research, rights, and responsibility can make durable together.
3. The book connects that question to venture design, brand governance, and capital allocation.
4. Its proposed advantages remain conditional, and many experiments may never become independent companies.
5. Read it to examine what a useful product would need to become a lasting organization.

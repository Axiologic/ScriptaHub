# Revocable Nobility — complete replacement proposal

Distinguish choosing capable people from granting a finite office, compare unavailable review with permanent audition on one imagined long project, then make independent evidence and reversible public explanation the reasons to read the contract and human coda.

## The office is not the person

1. Revocable Nobility asks how societies can empower capable leaders without making their offices permanent.
2. The book proposes temporary, domain-specific authority attached to service and higher accountability.
3. Selection matters, but its central question concerns the contract that governs whoever wins.
4. Its human-AI inquiry uses conditional simulations, rather than certifying a rightful ruling class.
5. Readers examine how correction can remain possible without making difficult leadership impossible.

Art: Open at a modern public-infrastructure planning room overlooking an unfinished pedestrian bridge. A project leader coordinates workers and an actual staged construction plan. A wall-mounted mandate has a visible beginning and ending on a calendar; the leader can leave while the office, archive and unfinished task remain. Keep the project useful and ordinary rather than a palace or tokenized throne. Color identity: copper structural steel, deep indigo plans, pale limestone, bright coral worker equipment and a turquoise landscape. This is an explicitly conceptual infrastructure illustration of the source’s long-project problem, not a named bridge, case study or engineering procedure.

Emotion: Respect for difficult service opens into curiosity about a finite mandate; no theatrical villain or hereditary crown.

## Two ways to lose correction

1. Imagine a long infrastructure project under two badly designed rules.
2. Under ceremonial accountability, the leader controls the records used to review that leadership.
3. The review cannot reliably challenge a warning the institution has made inaccessible.
4. Under constant removal threats, even an unpopular necessary decision can end the mandate.
5. The project stalls as leaders protect themselves from every difficult choice.
6. The source's comparison asks what due process and independent evidence change.

Art: Use two sequential views of the same unfinished bridge and planning office, not parallel generic icons. First an engineer’s warning is filed in an archive whose only key remains with the incumbent. A reviewer requests it and receives a curated progress picture while the actual warning remains visible behind the office partition. Then reset as a second hypothetical contract: the leader is repeatedly called away from the long work plan to defend each contested step, work crews remain waiting beside unplaced structural sections, and the schedule cannot advance. Do not use a floating bell, empty stamped paper or an invented numerical model chart. The contrast is between unusable review and action-destroying interruption; do not imply every protest or adverse review is illegitimate.

Emotion: First frustration at blocked scrutiny, then anxiety at permanent audition; neither extreme earns a triumphant verdict.

## A warning must survive its author

1. Its proposed contract separates a good-faith error from deliberate concealment.
2. Warnings survive independently, and review follows conditions declared before the conflict.
3. The officeholder can answer the evidence instead of facing immediate public punishment.
4. Power can end while the person retains dignity and ordinary life.
5. These safeguards remain a research proposal, whose modeled advantages depend on explicit assumptions.

Art: Change to a close independent project archive and review encounter. The engineer deposits the same warning in an independently held record; the incumbent cannot remove it. A pre-existing review condition is consulted, then the officeholder supplies an answer alongside the preserved evidence, rather than receiving an instant guilty stamp. Keep explanation and deliberate destruction of records visually distinct: an attempted missing-page edit cannot alter the retained copy. Show a possible end of mandate through return of the office access badge, while the person takes personal belongings and joins ordinary family or civic life; avoid harm to relatives or confiscating unrelated possessions. Review is explicitly conceptual, not an implemented legal procedure or a declaration that the illustrated leader is guilty. There is no empirical success score.

Emotion: Tension gives way to a usable distinction and humane descent, without celebrating punishment or guaranteeing ideal institutions.

## A public promise you can inspect

1. Yet citizens have work and families, not unlimited time to inspect institutional complexity.
2. The book proposes short public commitments that open onto a deeper shared record.
3. A simplified explanation should lead to sources, responsibility, uncertainty, and a genuine appeal.
4. Start with The Contract That Selects After Selection, then the final human coda.
5. Read it to examine how an accountable office could remain both capable and intelligible.

Art: Move to an inhabited evening kitchen: an adult balances paid work and care, faced with a deep pile of public project records. A short explanation of the same bridge mandate becomes a usable entry point: selecting its review condition opens the preserved warning and the named independent reviewer, not an unrelated glossy summary. The official and independent views use the same underlying record; no personalized contradictory truth. A clear appeal route reaches another person. Finish with the book’s actual contract chapter and Human Coda, beside the visible record connection; do not transform complexity into a magic one-click consent or completed legal outcome. This adopts the source’s reversible-explanation relationship without copying its statistical charts.

Emotion: Recognize finite attention without condescension; end with curiosity and the ability to ask a precise question.

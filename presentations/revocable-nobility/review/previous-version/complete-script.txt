Power that can end
Revocable Nobility asks how elite authority can end before disaster becomes its only auditor.
The book starts from the need for capable leadership, rather than denying hierarchy.
A human and AI inquiry tests different contracts around power and status.
Its models offer conditional hints, never a machine-certified constitution or forecast.
The reader examines whether office can remain answerable without turning every disagreement into removal.

Selection is only the beginning
One experiment changes the question from who wins office to what office rewards afterward.
Ceremonial accountability can protect a leader who controls the records and interpretation of failure.
Constant removal threats can create a different failure by making every difficult decision unsafe.
The book compares these conditions through explicit model assumptions rather than claiming measured future outcomes.
Its central distinction is between choosing a person and constraining the authority that person receives.

The machine cannot certify legitimacy
The wider inquiry connects scarcity, status, surveillance, mobility, and the cognitive burden of public judgment.
Its narrator describes provisional interpretations from a human and AI collaboration.
The final human coda asks how complex institutions can remain intelligible enough to contest.
Technical sophistication cannot itself establish consent or grant the machine a final political word.
Those limits keep the proposal open to criticism instead of announcing a perfected elite.

Read the contract behind rank
Begin with the author’s note and the method explaining what the models can show.
Then follow the distinction between selection and the contract that continues after selection.
The full inquiry tests the conditions under which honorable leadership could remain genuinely removable.
Its value lies in examining how privilege might end without turning correction into destruction.
Read the human coda to confront the unresolved burden that elegant institutional models leave behind.

## Care and the cost of refusal

One composite scene follows a grieving woman supported by her religious community.
Neighbors bring food and organize the ceremony when she cannot manage.
Years later, that community pressures her daughter to abandon a forbidden relationship.
The same network can sustain a person and make refusal painfully costly.
The book follows this tension into institutions that authorize power or resist it.

Art: An inhabited domestic threshold replaces the prior abstract authority figures: neighbors arriving with a covered meal, a bereaved person seated with hands at rest, another neighbor helping with ceremony preparation. Emotion: Grief and care → belonging → pressure and costly hesitation.

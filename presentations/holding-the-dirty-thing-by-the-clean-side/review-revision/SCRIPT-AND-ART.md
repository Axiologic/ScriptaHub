## 1 — The explanation and the cost

1. Calling an exhausting shift flexible does not give the worker back their night.
2. Holding the Dirty Thing by the Clean Side asks who pays for respectable explanations.
3. Its distinctive test follows the burden, even when an institution serves a real purpose.
4. The book connects public authority, emotional resilience, and algorithmic decisions through that question.
5. It asks readers to separate necessary cooperation from costs made difficult to challenge.

Art: Establish a small working bakery at night, not a generic meeting room.
Indigo window with a small moon, terracotta oven, wheeled brass bread rack,
cream loaves and flour-blue apron establish the scene. One baker and a shift
coordinator have ordinary, recognizable faces. The baker guides a supported
rack toward a shop hatch; the coordinator checks the same work rota. Show the
work's useful destination, not an evil manager smiling at a victim. The rack
wheels remain on the floor and hands meet its handle. The book title appears
in narration; only the short player heading appears above the scene.

Emotion: recognition of a familiar euphemism, then curiosity about the book's
more difficult distinction between useful cooperation and concealed costs.

## 2 — Who receives the night

1. Imagine a bakery calling its new night rota an efficiency improvement.
2. Bread still needs to reach the shop each morning.
3. But the same baker now carries more of the disrupted sleep.
4. Management's label cannot tell us whether that distribution is necessary.
5. The book's burden test asks who benefits, who bears costs, and who can object.
6. Some constraints are real; their current allocation still needs justification.

Art: Close view of the rota mounted beside the actual oven, with the same
baker's portrait identifying their row. An old and revised schedule are
visually distinct versions. Move a moon-marked shift into that row while
retaining total scheduled work; do not imply that renaming alone added the
shift. The heading then changes to “Efficiency” while the revised night
allocation remains identical. Pull the frame wide enough to retain the
morning shop hatch and bread rack: demand is real, allocation is the question.
Use a small number of large cells and symbols, no illegible calendar or score.
The baker points to their row; the coordinator turns toward it. No automatic
agreement, compensation or completed labor negotiation.

Emotion: the useful shared task remains visible while attention moves to the
particular person carrying its cost.

## 3 — Calm is not a correction

1. The emotional chapters ask what happens when resilience replaces a response to exhaustion.
2. Support can help someone face pressure without making that pressure acceptable.
3. In our example, a calmer conversation leaves the night rota unchanged.
4. AI could intensify this distance when a score appears to make the decision.
5. The book also asks whether automation could remove some degrading work instead.

Art: Same two people seated briefly beside the working bakery, shoulders and
hands relaxing during conversation; the rota remains in full view and keeps
exactly the revised assignment. Do not show counseling as inherently useless
or hostile. At the AI sentence, a proposed scheduling screen displays the same
allocation and refers to a score without a fabricated number. The coordinator
still exists beside it: responsibility has not literally vanished. Then a
separate, clearly labelled proposed lift is sketched beside the actual rack.
Keep it visibly a proposal; no performed miracle, vanished worker or claim
that automation has already fixed nights, wages or fatigue. The two possible
uses of technology are contrasting questions within one workplace.

Emotion: relief from being heard, then recognition that calm did not change
the allocation; a conditional constructive possibility follows the unease.

## 4 — Read beyond the polished words

1. Its constructive proposals make burdens visible and give affected people routes to challenge them.
2. An attractive report is insufficient if the baker cannot question the schedule.
3. The point is not to prove that all institutions or ideals are fraudulent.
4. Begin with the clean-side rule, then follow its tests into emotional and algorithmic authority.
5. Read it to examine one respectable explanation whose costs you have stopped noticing.

Art: Change to an overhead worktop composition. The exact rota, a record of
night allocation and an open book are physical items, rather than a dashboard.
The baker's actual objection marker travels from their row to a review request;
the governing schedule remains pending, not automatically rewritten. The book
opens at “The Clean-Side Rule”; a folded tab marks the emotional chapter.

with the worker still an agent who can ask, not an icon beneath a slogan.
Hands must have connected sleeves/forearms and supported objects. No floating

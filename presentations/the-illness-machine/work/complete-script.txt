A cause beyond the clinic
The Illness Machine asks how social arrangements enter the causes and persistence of mental distress.
It investigates causal pathways rather than claiming society explains every disorder.
The book compares different kinds of evidence and confronts findings inconvenient to its thesis.
Its practical tension is between treating suffering and changing conditions that keep producing exposure.
Readers encounter an inquiry into institutional responsibility that preserves the importance of clinical care.

Ask what the evidence can show
The opening follows a change in family income that became an unexpected research opportunity.
The book uses it to ask which social changes can help distinguish causation from association.
Different research designs provide different leverage, and none makes every setting interchangeable.
Biological vulnerability remains part of the inquiry rather than an inconvenient fact to exclude.
This evidential discipline matters because an attractive social explanation can still be too broad.

Care and exposure are different levers
A clinic can help a distressed person while harmful conditions continue elsewhere.
The book examines institutions that distribute exposure, shape interpretation, or make recovery harder.
Its case against itself confronts evidence that resists a simple social account.
The constructive chapters consider prevention alongside treatment, without promising a world free of mental illness.
Readers encounter a question about causal responsibility rather than a substitute for individual clinical judgment.

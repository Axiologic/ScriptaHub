# The Future of Research Infrastructure — targeted example and art revision

Status: proposed full script before voice. Four revised sentences preserve sixteen recordings.

Spine: When models change, one research question must retain its baseline, exact data, failed runs, disputed interpretations and accepted decisions; the book develops a transaction architecture that keeps those stages and authorities distinct.

Reader gain: Learn what a scientific project must retain beyond a successful model answer, using the source regularization/distribution-shift research episode.

## Complete proposed speech

### Scene 1
1. The Future of Research Infrastructure asks what must persist when research models keep changing.
2. Its answer centers on a scientific project state that connects claims, evidence, and decisions.
3. The book follows how proposed work becomes executed, evaluated, and accepted knowledge.
4. Documents and agents become participants around that shared record, rather than its only memory.
5. Readers can examine a research architecture designed for continuity, accountability, and replaceable tools.

### Scene 2
1. Its regularization example asks whether a method improves robustness under a specific distribution shift.
2. The project keeps the baseline, dataset version, proposal, and comparison criteria attached to that question.
3. A failed run differs from an experiment that runs but shows no improvement.
4. Competing evaluations remain visible before anyone changes the project's accepted claim.
5. This is the architectural difference the book develops beyond a larger collection of research tools.

### Scene 3
1. A proposed analysis is not yet an executed analysis or an accepted scientific result.
2. The book separates proposal, execution, evaluation, and commitment within a research transaction.
3. Different tools and people retain different responsibilities and guarantees.
4. Human authority remains visible at consequential transitions instead of disappearing inside an autonomous workflow.
5. The design aims to preserve scientific meaning as more work becomes machine assisted.

### Scene 4
1. The implementation discussion recommends a narrow scientific episode supported from beginning to end.
2. A pilot would need to demonstrate value before adding more speculative capabilities.
3. The book also asks whether its architecture improves on simpler existing arrangements.
4. Read the opening architecture alongside the chapters explaining each responsibility it introduces.
5. The Future of Research Infrastructure offers a proposed instrument for maintaining research beyond individual outputs.

## Original art plan

Identity: a computational research studio with navy paper, orange experiment tabs and lavender/teal distribution samples. The persistent scientific question is a large clipped protocol, not a terminal dashboard. Shifted input samples are schematic and deliberately have no scores or fitted performance curve.

1. A scientist opens the same regularization question beside two clearly labeled input collections, training and shifted evaluation. Changing tool notebooks at the edge leave the central question, baseline and dataset identity in place. This is continuity of a research episode, not a model-company replacement animation.
2. A close experiment folio preserves its approved protocol and exact data version. Two alternative run records become visible: an incomplete run with no scientific observation, versus a completed run with a negative observation. These are source-described possible outcomes, explicitly labeled alternatives, not fabricated observed findings. The result records retain their protocol attachment. A statistical reviewer and a method reviewer leave separate questions; one does not overwrite the other's view.
3. At a shared review encounter, the same candidate claim arrives with execution receipt and differing evaluations. A human places a 'Keep disputed' marker while preserving raw output. The proposed accepted-state page stays unchanged. This illustrates one legitimate source outcome and preserves human/institutional configured authority, rather than suggesting every run must end with positive acceptance.
4. The scientist traces this single episode through the retained protocol, run record, disagreement and decision before evaluating a narrow pilot against ordinary tools. A side-by-side pilot/ordinary-workflow comparison has no winner or invented efficiency scores. Reading route points to Chapter1 architecture, Chapters3/6 state and review, Chapter11 falsifiable pilot.

# The Predator’s Niche

## Trust is the habitat

The Predator's Niche examines predation that succeeds by appearing to cooperate.

Its nonfiction inquiry treats trust as both a social achievement and an exploitable resource.

The scope connects psychopathic traits, organizational incentives, and artificial agents.

The book distinguishes research, inference, and speculation rather than treating them as interchangeable evidence.

Its question is how cooperative systems can remain open without rewarding those who exploit them.

That framing directs attention toward environments and behavior rather than a simple catalogue of villains.

## The organization need not share one mind

One department can know about suffering while another sees only performance indicators.

The book asks how those fragments can produce harmful behavior without one complete intention.

It calls this organizational pattern egregopathy, presenting a proposed analytical concept.

The inquiry follows where information, incentives, and responsibility stop meeting.

That expands the problem beyond identifying an unusually callous individual.

The reader can examine how a cooperative institution makes correction possible or systematically ineffective.

## The machine analogy has conditions

The discussion of artificial agents concerns enacted policies, not claims about a machine's feelings.

An unemotional optimizer is not automatically placed inside the proposed category.

The relevant pattern combines exploiting vulnerabilities, managing impressions, and resisting meaningful correction.

The book also rejects a biological division of present humanity into two moral species.

These limits protect an interesting comparison from becoming a convenient accusation.

The broader inquiry asks what designs let cooperation survive without making trust defenseless.

## Begin with the evidence levels

Start with the epistemic note, then The Animal That Hunts Trust.

The opening establishes why the relevant habitat is a network of social expectations.

This book suits readers interested in institutional behavior and the governance of capable agents.

Its distinctive benefit is connecting individual strategies with the conditions that amplify them.

The argument is most useful when its categories remain specific and open to challenge.

Reading gives you a way to investigate predation without abandoning the value of cooperation.

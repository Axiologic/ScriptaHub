# Coherence Pressure — proposed complete revision

Status: root source-reviewed draft; independent review required before new voice/public replacement.
Purpose: book introduction, measured 2–3 minutes, English local Piper, one sentence per beat, ordinary 1.45-second gaps with longer transitions.

## Narrative promise

WHY: an explanation can preserve plausible individual sentences while inventing their connection.
HOW: the book reconstructs a documented human-model writing exchange, where two childhood observations become one attractive explanatory frame before their relationship is demonstrated.
WHAT: readers can inspect the original record, the correction, and a proposed research programme that also puts the book's own term at risk of rejection.

Do not turn this into a child-development lesson, a diagnosis, or a claim that the model has an established internal psychological mechanism. Preserve useful synthesis and the possibility of genuine relations.

## Complete spoken script

### Scene 1 — When the connection outruns evidence
1. Coherence Pressure examines a mistake that can survive accurate individual sentences.
2. An explanation joins observations more strongly than the available evidence allows.
3. This book reconstructs a documented conversation that turned two childhood behaviours into one theory.
4. Readers can inspect the original exchange, the correction, and the proposed research programme.
5. Its question is how to preserve useful synthesis without manufacturing a connection.

### Scene 2 — Two observations, one attractive story
1. A parent described a child wanting an unwanted app removed from a tablet.
2. The same child could keep watching short videos as their format became repetitive.
3. Asked about a connection, the model organized both around the cost of ignoring.
4. That memorable comparison gave a shared explanation more weight than the evidence supported.
5. The book distinguishes a common descriptive language from a demonstrated common mechanism.

### Scene 3 — A correction must change the conclusion
1. After the parent's challenge, the model separated avoidance from continued exploration of possible rewards.
2. The comparison remained possible, but a shared mechanism had not been demonstrated.
3. The book then turns its scrutiny on the name given to this mistake.
4. Coherence pressure remains a working hypothesis, which existing concepts might already explain.
5. That second risk makes the book more than a collection of model failures.

### Scene 4 — Read the record, test the idea
1. Its proposed research programme compares source material with the connections added by an explanation.
2. Good tests must preserve justified synthesis, alongside cases where the answer remains uncertain.
3. The appendices retain the exchange so readers can challenge the author's interpretation.
4. Begin there, then follow the analysis into the chapters on measurement and falsification.
5. Read to ask which connection an explanation added, and what would justify it.

## Art direction

Domestic terracotta, soft oat, aubergine ink and restrained electric cyan from the tablet. Flat quiet backgrounds in all themes. Four distinctly staged compositions: editorial incident desk; physical home scene; close-up revision; reading and experimental comparison. No brain diagram, glowing AI face, common neurological gate, diagnosis icon, scales, trophy, giant abstract bridge, or repeated presenter plus three symbols. Only one confident player heading; bundled Red Hat fonts. Keep action extents clear of subtitles.

The child and parent are illustrative casting for the source's anonymized observation, not portraits. The child is a small full-body figure in a turquoise sweater with short dark hair, supported by a stool/sofa as appropriate; parent wears a terracotta shirt. Stable identities. Expressions should be mild annoyance, anticipation, then adult scrutiny; no pathologizing, frightening or mocking the child. Repeated videos are abstract coloured moving shapes, never actual social-media clips or brands.

### Scene 1 — the incident is a writing problem

Close desk view with parent reading the source conversation on a supported tablet beside a printed manuscript. Begin with a short labelled record “Conversation”, then “Draft explanation” as the parent follows a line with a pencil. The printed draft has two visible short source-note phrases, “Remove the app” and “Continue the feed”; these are paraphrased labels, not verbatim quotations. A movable copper bracket appears connecting them and is labelled “Proposed link”. This bracket represents the model's explanatory move, not proof or a physical connection inside the child.

At line3 widen to show parent examining rather than celebrating the draft. At line4 the pencil moves back from the bracket to the conversation record; line5 the parent pauses, leaving the bracket open. Do not animate smashing a theory or declaring all connections impossible. The visible disagreement is whether this link was earned.
Emotional arc: curiosity → recognition → active doubt.
Checks: pointer/bracket endpoint targets exactly the two notes; bracket never connects to child/brain; parent hand and pencil remain connected, record stays supported.

### Scene 2 — observed actions before theory

Change to a full domestic scene at child scale, with a low sofa/table and supported tablet. First show a stationary unwanted generic app tile while the child pushes the tablet gently toward the parent and points at that tile. The tile does not launch itself and does not threaten the child. The action is a request for removal, not a depicted successful deletion the source did not report.

After the first line, change tablet view to an abstract short-video feed. The same child looks expectantly and makes two distinct swipes: the outer frame repeats, while its inner coloured shape changes. Their body settles between swipes. This is a reconstruction of the reported observation, not a controlled experiment demonstrating addiction or a mechanism. Do not put app rejection and video attraction on opposite sides of a literal gate.

At line3 pull back to the adult's writing desk holding two small stills of the actions; the model's manuscript bracket joins the written descriptions. Reveal “Cost of ignoring” on the manuscript, clearly under “Proposed link”. At line4 the adult follows the bracket with the pencil; line5 the pencil rests beside a new note “Evidence for this link?” rather than crossing out the observations.
Emotional arc: ordinary annoyance → low-key anticipation → intellectual attraction → scrutiny.
Checks: child's feet meet floor/stool; tablet meets lap/table or both hands; pointing finger meets tile; swiping is two purposeful gestures, not endless decorative motion. Keep parent scale consistent across shots. Written comparison remains an authored proposal, never validated anatomy.

### Scene 3 — revise the relationship

Large close-up of the same manuscript, showing the two source notes and bracket. A hand lifts the bracket strip off the page and sets it beside the still-visible observations. Under the first note show “Avoidance”; under the second “Exploration”. A small annotation “Mechanism unresolved” replaces the former unqualified join. The two observations stay intact and are not moved into unrelated boxes. This is an explanatory enactment of the correction, not a claim that the book's exact page has movable paper strips.

At line3 the manuscript turns to a working title card “Coherence pressure” with a pencil underline. The same reviewer hand writes “Working hypothesis” immediately beneath it. Reuse the same questioning gesture that was applied to the first theory: the second label receives no halo or authoritative stamp. Do not erase it as disproven. A small original source record remains beside the page so the hypothesis has a documented origin without appearing validated.
Emotional arc: clarification → relief → self-correction → measured curiosity.
Checks: bracket removed without source-note disappearance; contact hand moves with strip; annotation does not crowd source labels; hypothesis title is part of the material being examined, separate from player heading.

### Scene 4 — a reader can inspect the joins

A reader's open copy rests on a table; a narrow foldout incident record sits beside it. Show the actual appendix heading “The Complete Incident File”, then turn toward “CoherencePressureBench”. The latter is an actual proposed programme name, not an operating service.

One source pair and one generated explanation are compared in a close reading insert. A removable line between two small source marks is considered alongside the explanation. First its supporting passage is visible; then the page turns to a different case whose support is unresolved, and the line ends in a question mark. These are schematic representations of the protocol's contrasting cases, not fake benchmark results. Do not imply that rejecting every connection is success. No scores, performance graph or completed detector.

At line3 return to the incident record, with the reader's index finger following the actual exchange. At line4 a simple page tab “Measurement” becomes visible; end with the source and interpretation side by side, pencil at rest. Make the final action an invitation to inspect, not an automated verdict.
Emotional arc: investigative interest → discrimination → independent agency.
Checks: each case appears sequentially with no claim of measurement; page/record fully supported; real page turn and attached forearms; final book remains readable at phone scale and avoids caption collisions.

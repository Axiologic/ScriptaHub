# The Permission Paradox — same-prototype continuity revision

Status: root-reviewed complete speech; two revised sentences preserve eighteen clips.

Spine: An unfamiliar idea needs evidence to obtain resources and resources to create evidence; Longview proposes converting conditional possibility into a small, stoppable test whose learning can change the next funding decision.

Reader gain: Distinguish belief in an unconventional claim from support for a bounded experiment, and see why an informative failure belongs in the funding record.

## Complete proposed speech

### Scene 1
1. The Permission Paradox examines ideas evaluated through categories built from the past.
2. It asks how institutions can test unfamiliar possibilities without rewarding persuasive speculation.
3. AI sharpens the problem when candidate novelty outpaces society's capacity to evaluate it.
4. The book proposes conditional review, bounded inquiry, and funding arrangements that purchase learning.
5. Readers gain a way to inspect this tension without dividing people into visionaries and fools.

### Scene 2
1. Imagine an inventor proposing a leak sensor whose performance has not yet been tested.
2. An evaluator usually reasons forward from existing evidence, precedent, and recognized categories.
3. Both approaches can be reasonable while producing a persistent mismatch.
4. The book also recognizes that an unfamiliar idea may simply be wrong.
5. Its question is how to distinguish responsible testing from either premature rejection or technological credulity.

### Scene 3
1. Longview is the book's proposed approach to conditional assessment of future potential.
2. A small, controlled test could show whether that sensor detects the leak.
3. Reversibility, meaningful benefit, protected participants, and learning value remain important conditions.
4. The funding discussion connects short experimental cycles with patience for difficult research.
5. These are institutional proposals to investigate, not automatic reasons to approve every unconventional claim.

### Scene 4
1. Begin with the mismatch between candidate novelty and the capacity to evaluate it.
2. Follow the review and funding proposals together, since each changes what the other can accomplish.
3. The book asks institutions to examine the costs of delay alongside the risks of action.
4. The Permission Paradox gives that tension a specific framework and a proposed research programme.
5. Read it to consider when a responsible experiment can teach more than another ranking.

## Original art plan

Identity: a small workshop and public review counter, burnt-orange prototype hardware, petrol-blue closed test loop, pale mint observation sheet and lavender reviewer attire. Expressive innovator and evaluator share the same tangible prototype rather than occupying unrelated icon rows.

The prototype is an explicitly editorial, hypothetical acoustic leak sensor. The book does not contain this device or claim sensor novelty. Its only role is to make a modest claim, a controlled test and a negative observation visible. No detection rate, claimed technology advance or field use is invented. The test uses a closed tabletop loop, no people exposed, a stop valve, and a predeclared question: does this sensor detect this controlled small leak?

1. At a review counter the innovator brings that rough sensor and a short claim. The evaluator has previous evidence categories but the sensor has an empty test record. The same blank record physically remains with the device, making the evidence/resources loop visible without treating the evaluator as foolish or the innovator as correct.
2. Close view of the claim and apparatus: the innovator points to possible leak detection, while the evaluator points to the currently empty observation field. Both inspect the same object from different directions. The device may fail; no glowing success halo or visionary privilege.
3. A smaller bench test replaces a request for general deployment. The closed loop gets one controlled leak; the sensor's output stays unchanged. The observation sheet records 'This leak was missed'. That is a hypothetical negative observation, not a source finding or a proof that all future versions fail. The stop valve closes, the record returns with the same sensor, and a small evidence tranche has purchased a specific reason to revise or stop.
4. Back at the same counter, the initial claim and negative observation remain side by side. The human reviewer keeps the deployment request unapproved and marks the failed assumption for revision; continued funding is not guaranteed. A Failure Ledger retains what was learned, while the book route returns to second-order review, reversible inquiry and the epistemic clock.

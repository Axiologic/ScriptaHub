When output stops proving knowledge
The Great Decoupling asks what remains reliable when impressive output stops proving deep understanding.
AI assistance can separate an artifact’s quality from the competence that institutions assume produced it.
The book follows that break through work, education, culture, and delegated judgment.
Its constructive direction is to reconnect useful assistance with learning and genuine responsibility.
Readers can distinguish becoming more productive from remaining able to understand and challenge consequential decisions.

The artifact conceals its maker’s limits
An employee can produce a convincing artifact with assistance while understanding only part of its reasoning.
Its visible quality no longer tells an institution everything it once assumed about the maker.
The book treats this as a change in evidence, responsibility, and the organization of expertise.
A useful result can coexist with uncertainty about who could detect its important failure.
That distinction carries the argument beyond a forecast of which jobs disappear.

Assistance should preserve the learner
The later chapters examine what happens when practice is removed along with routine work.
A profession can consume existing expertise while weakening the pathway that produces its next generation.
The proposed right to remain capable concerns learning, explanation, refusal, and real authority to correct.
It does not demand that everyone perform every task alone.
Readers are asked to inspect the form of delegation rather than accept or reject assistance wholesale.

Read what must stay connected
Begin with the first chapter’s account of the separation between output and understanding.
Follow that distinction into the chapters on work, education, and accumulated cognitive identity.
The full argument compares opportunities with the dependencies that better artifacts can conceal.
Its central reading question is who remains able to answer when delegated production fails.
Read it to distinguish an effective assistant from an arrangement that leaves its user less capable.

The title is not a guarantee
Why the World Won’t End offers conditional hope without claiming catastrophe is impossible.
It separates the end of a familiar world from the extinction of humanity.
The book takes serious hazards seriously while questioning the certainty that apocalypse stories encourage.
Its central concern is what a civilization must preserve so that learning can continue after disruption.
Readers gain a way to examine danger without confusing survival with a good future.

Mechanisms have different clocks
A sudden catastrophe and a slow loss of adaptive capacity create different problems.
The book compares hazards through their mechanisms rather than ranking them by dramatic appeal.
It asks what can harm many people, break technological continuity, or threaten the species itself.
These categories must stay distinct if preparation is to match the actual danger.
The comparison turns an emotional argument about the end into a more specific inquiry.

There is no complete cockpit
The institutional chapters examine decisions made when no leader holds the whole system in mind.
Distributed knowledge makes civilization capable, while fragmented responsibility can make correction difficult.
Resilience may preserve useful abilities without preserving freedom or humane social arrangements.
The book therefore connects preparedness with dissent, memory, pluralism, and the capacity to rebuild.
Its adolescent-species image remains a metaphor for uneven maturity, rather than a biological prediction.

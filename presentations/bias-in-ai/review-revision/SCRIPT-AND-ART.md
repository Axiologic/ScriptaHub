# Bias in AI — retain script, sharpen proxy and stage the paths

Status: approved and installed with measured English narration and final all-theme QA.

Story spine: This course extends bias beyond training data by revealing unequal resources behind similar agent replies, then makes measurement error concrete through medical spending as a proxy for need; readers learn to identify the actual harmful dependency before selecting a metric.

## Complete proposed speech

### Bias in AI
1. Bias in AI explains why fairness problems cannot be reduced to biased training data.
2. Its course follows harmful pathways through measurement, language models, retrieval, and agent decisions.
3. The central lesson is to identify what is distorted before choosing a metric.
4. Different fairness criteria protect different interests and can conflict.
5. Readers learn to examine consequential dependencies without expecting one universal fairness score.

### Different paths, similar answers
1. Two matched users can receive similar final answers after very different agent journeys.
2. One may receive additional research, a tool call, or escalation to a human.
3. The other may receive none of those resources.
4. The book treats those choices as allocations that ordinary text benchmarks can miss.
5. That example explains why its account extends from model behavior to complete workflows.

### Name what the measure protects
1. A predictive target can reflect an institution's assumptions rather than the outcome people actually need.
2. Medical spending can reflect access to care, instead of how much care someone needs.
3. When access differs, using spending as the target can reproduce that inequality.
4. That is why the book asks whose need a chosen measure actually represents.
5. Institutions remain responsible for choosing those standards, even when a model predicts them accurately.

### Read beyond the bias score
1. Start with the distinctions between statistical bias, harmful disparities, and normative disagreement.
2. Then follow how retrieval, memory, and agents create additional pathways to unequal outcomes.
3. The final chapters examine evidence that must be renewed after system changes.
4. Bias in AI is useful because it keeps the object of evaluation explicit.
5. Read it to investigate particular harmful dependencies and judge whether a proposed intervention addresses them.

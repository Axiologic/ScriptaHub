# Hunger After All the Worlds

## Imagination needs an outside

Hunger After All the Worlds combines a novel with an atlas of speculation.

Its powerful world-makers face a problem that greater capability cannot automatically solve.

How can imagination encounter something it has not already reduced to itself?

The book explores that tension through inhabited worlds and a revisable conceptual table.

The table classifies operations, not merely famous devices or familiar story decorations.

That approach offers readers a way to compare how invented worlds produce consequences.

## An operation changes a world

Changing gravity and distributing one identity across bodies are different speculative operations.

Their importance lies in what the change makes possible or difficult.

The book organizes such operations into families and combinations.

An abundant library, for example, can make selection scarcer than information.

This method connects an imaginative premise with the social problem it creates.

It is a working grammar whose categories remain open to revision.

## The inhabitants can disagree

The novel's visits give the conceptual atlas a difficult human counterpart.

A local inhabitant does not exist merely to illustrate the visitor's theory.

The encounters preserve deadlines, relationships, and costs that a classification might flatten.

Memorial references also keep the genealogy of speculative ideas visible.

The form therefore moves between literary inheritance, conceptual comparison, and lived resistance.

Its richest question is what escapes an observer's increasingly capable model.

## Enter the atlas through its people

Begin with the explanation of the table, then enter the first memorial encounter.

The two modes show why the book needs both system and story.

Readers interested in worldbuilding can trace how operations combine into new pressures.

Readers interested in fiction can question the authority of the world-maker.

Neither route requires treating the table as a final inventory of imagination.

The book invites invention that remains attentive to what its categories cannot contain.

# Eden Before Mars — complete replacement proposal

Bring the conditional off-world engineering comparison to an explicitly imagined public path, expose a stopped machine’s dependency on parts and service information, then connect ecological limits and the right to challenge a plan with the book’s proposed constitution of constructive capacity.

## The question comes home

1. Eden Before Mars asks why technologies for hostile worlds should change expectations here on Earth.
2. If autonomous construction matures, ordinary towns could gain new ways to repair and build.
3. Earth already has air, water, infrastructure, and people with claims to the land.
4. The book's challenge is to expand local capability without enclosing life behind new gatekeepers.
5. It joins technological history with an explicitly conditional proposal for a more inhabitable world.

Art: Begin on an ordinary inhabited square with a broken uneven path, useful mature shade tree, homes and adults discussing its repair. In a small clearly marked conditional comparison, a robotic vehicle beside a simple unoccupied off-world habitat handles local material; do not depict an existing Mars settlement or a launch forecast. Bring focus back to Earth’s existing tree, water supply and neighbors with different uses of the square. A supported dormant construction machine and salvaged stone represent a possible capacity to improve it. Burnt-orange equipment, olive trees, turquoise water, warm limestone and violet civic clothing create an original daylight identity. No completed utopia or uniform grid of garden icons.

Emotion: A distant technical ambition turns into a nearby civic possibility, then into a question about people and place.

## A machine can bring dependence

1. Imagine a town reusing stone for a shaded public path.
2. A robotic machine might reduce the work of handling irregular materials.
3. But one failed component can stop the work while the material remains available.
4. If only one supplier can repair it, the town's freedom depends on that supplier.
5. The book asks who controls construction after the machine makes building cheaper.

Art: Close into the same explicitly imagined public-path project. Salvaged irregular stones sit in a supported stack beside the existing mature tree and an inactive compact machine. Show a distinct failed component in the machine’s maintenance bay and the corresponding empty spare compartment; never give a live repair procedure. The crew can inspect a general service diagram, but the matching replacement part and service information remain with a single supplier. The crew’s request reaches that supplier without a promised delivery. No floating key, abstract gate or dialogue pretending the issue is solved. Keep the machine clearly stopped, people clear of moving equipment, and the path unfinished. This is a hypothetical illustration of the book’s maintenance-chain and right-to-repair arguments, not a documented equipment failure.

Emotion: Practical possibility meets a small but consequential interruption, exposing an institutional dependency inside a material tool.

## Earth is already alive

1. Difficulties also remain when the machinery is available and the community agrees.
2. A proposed water feature still needs a water budget and room for living systems.
3. More garden-like settlements should reduce pressure on wild places, rather than landscape everything.
4. The historical chapters explain why technical power has repeatedly supported cooperation and domination.
5. That history makes repair, oversight, and the freedom to refuse part of the design.

Art: Change to a landscape planning view around the square and an adjoining living wetland. A proposed ornamental water feature has a visibly finite source, and its footprint initially conflicts with the established vegetation corridor. The discussion narrows the proposed footprint without instantly creating new habitat, water or mature trees. Keep the existing wild area distinct from an intensively tended town garden. At the historical sentence, retain the SAME present imagined town and its water plan. Show the actual allocation/maintenance record belonging to this hypothetical project and a resident asking to inspect it; an official’s sole custody initially limits that scrutiny. This is explicitly the present invented example of the book’s historical pattern, not an old artifact or pseudo-historical episode. No road remains while the visual concerns records. Retain material/ecological limits rather than turning the image into a universal ideal landscape.

Emotion: The focus widens from the machine to ecological limits and the inhabitants who can contest a plan.

## The garden needs a constitution

1. The proposed constitution gives communities capacity to build and obligations to maintain.
2. Residents need reasons for decisions, alternative providers, and ways to challenge plans.
3. A repairable machine helps only when the parts, knowledge, and authority are accessible.
4. Begin with the Planetary Mirror Test, then examine the Constitution of Constructive Capacity.
5. Read it to ask what a beautiful place must let its inhabitants control.

Art: Return to the stopped machine as an explicitly proposed local-maintenance arrangement. Two independent service options can inspect the same understandable documentation and compatible spare part. A resident’s concern about the wetland remains beside the project plan and can reach an independent reviewer; neither a provider’s approval nor a majority vote automatically overrides ecological limits. The scene does not perform a hazardous repair, resume the excavator instantly or finish the path. Close on the actual source book opened to the two named sections, with the maintenance obligation and protected living corridor still visible in the background. The promise is capacity plus contestability and care, not a guaranteed technical, legal or environmental outcome.

Emotion: The constructive release is access to real choices and continuing care, not a machine-made paradise.

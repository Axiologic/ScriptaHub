# RAG and EPR — one decision across the portfolio

Status: proposed script/art review; no voice or public changes.

Story spine: The book first teaches practical RAG, then asks whether a bounded complementary evidence set helps a researcher assess an idea better than individually relevant results; the source manuscript-review-system proposal makes missing components and comparison obligations concrete.

## Complete proposed speech

### RAG and EPR
1. RAG and EPR asks why individually relevant sources can form a weak evidence set.
2. Its first half builds a practical understanding of retrieval and grounded generation.
3. The second develops Evidence Portfolio Retrieval as a testable research proposal.
4. The proposed change is to select complementary evidence for a specific scientific decision.
5. Readers can compare that idea with modern retrieval methods before accepting its claimed value.

### Relevant together, weak together
1. Several retrieved documents can be individually relevant while repeating almost the same evidence.
2. A less similar source might contain the counterexample that changes the decision.
3. One example asks what already exists behind a proposed manuscript-review system.
4. Similarity remains useful, but it cannot describe every property of a useful evidence set.
5. That distinction motivates the proposed shift from isolated rankings toward portfolios.

### A portfolio has obligations
1. An evidence portfolio is selected for a stated task under an explicit budget.
2. Its members may provide support, criticism, boundary conditions, or alternative explanations.
3. Coverage, source dependence, and unresolved gaps remain part of the assessment.
4. Modern retrieval already includes related methods, so the proposal needs strong comparative baselines.
5. The book treats improved downstream decisions as a hypothesis to test, not an established advantage.

### Read the comparison
1. Begin with the engineering account of indexing, retrieval, context construction, and evaluation.
2. Then examine where independently ranked results fall short for a scientific decision.
3. The portfolio chapters explain an alternative representation and the comparisons needed to evaluate it.
4. RAG and EPR connects practical retrieval knowledge with a clearly bounded research question.
5. Read it to decide when better evidence selection requires thinking about the set.

## Original art direction
Identity: a research dossier being examined. Deep marine, terracotta, saffron page tabs, pale blue working notes. One thoughtful researcher with tied-back hair and one older methodological reviewer. Physical papers are authored documents with visible purpose; no random flask, chart or abstract performance bar.

1. A wide research reading desk, manuscript proposal open in the center and a constrained reference shelf behind it. The proposal's visible functions are manuscript assessment, source verification, provenance and reproducibility, with human judgment reserved. A researcher's hand follows these functions; retrieved material begins collecting to one side. The book's engineering-first/research-proposal-second identity is expressed by the reading desk, not a sales demo of software.
2. Close-up of that exact proposal beside three near-duplicate automated-review references. Their coverage marks all land on the same function, while provenance/reproducibility remain uncovered. Replacing one duplicate with a component-method source adds a new supported part; a separate question mark marks a still-unanswered challenge. Schematic papers do not invent real publications or findings. The decision remains what precedents and missing pieces must be considered before assessing the proposal.
3. The same dossier opens into a fixed four-place evidence holder: one precedent, one support method, one critique/boundary question, one unresolved gap. Roles are visibly provisional annotations rather than source truth stamps. Shared source lineage is shown by a common binder tab, separating textual variety from evidential independence. A finite holder makes attention budget physical. Do not imply every role can always be filled, or that four is the book's mandatory number.
4. Pull back to researcher and reviewer comparing dossiers under the same attention allowance. Strong ranked/diversified retrieval remains a real comparator, alongside the proposed role-aware dossier. The proposal's decision stays open and the reviewer's hand points to what a comparative test would need to resolve. Reading copy sits with the actual evidence. No rising bar or winner seal pretends the proposed benefit has been measured.

Headings are strong shared display typography; paper annotations are short and legible, used to identify mechanisms. Palette, paper scale, hands and views change by function; evidence location and the proposal's recognizable tabs preserve continuity. All art remains above the caption-safe boundary, with no duplicate slogan or decorative framing.

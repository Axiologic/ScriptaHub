## Complete spoken draft

### 1. Anti-Trivialization Machines of the Future

1. Anti-Trivialization Machines of the Future asks who protects judgment when convincing answers become cheap.
2. This institutional argument moves beyond telling distracted people to exercise more discipline.
3. It proposes attention rules that could make AI answerable to our declared purposes.
4. You gain ways to distinguish helpful assistance from systems competing to keep you occupied.

### 2. The interruption has rules

1. Consider the book's proposal for an agent governed by a person's attention policy.
2. During protected study, medical alerts could arrive immediately while workplace requests wait.
3. The person declares those priorities before another application demands an immediate response.
4. Applications would negotiate with that agent instead of competing directly for attention.
5. Protection comes from enforceable conditions, rather than repeatedly resisting each interruption alone.

### 3. Assistance has a principal

1. The book also asks whose interests the supposedly personal assistant actually serves.
2. An examination tutor should support learning, rather than secretly maximize time spent online.
3. Its main objectives should be inspectable, including conflicts with commercial incentives.
4. The user must also remain able to change goals and allow unexpected interruptions.
5. That makes protection a revisable delegation, rather than surrendering judgment to another gatekeeper.

### 4. The future needs time

1. The same argument expands from personal interruptions to institutions making consequential decisions.
2. Faster AI analysis does not make a forest grow or infrastructure age faster.
3. A proposed future-impact ledger preserves assumptions about long-term effects for later review.
4. Comparing those expectations with outcomes helps an institution learn after its staff have changed.

### 5. Protect judgment together

1. The book connects these designs with evidence histories, education, and institutions that check one another.
2. Its proposals remain contestable, including the rules imposed by attention-protection systems themselves.
3. The central shift is from producing more answers to preserving conditions for judgment.
4. Begin with The Attention Constitution for the concrete question of whom an assistant serves.
5. Follow Institutions for Time to see how that responsibility extends beyond the immediate request.

## Original art direction

Use illustrated civic architecture and tactile editorial mechanisms: vermilion, ink-blue, celadon, pale apricot and violet accents. Quiet backgrounds in all themes. Distinguish this from Cones' clinic paper-theatre and Limits' mechanical counter: warmer domestic scale initially, a full-stage typographic policy close-up, then seasonal civic landscape. One strong player title; no artwork slogans, tiny labels or arbitrary prop rows.

Stable character: a mature learner with curly dark hair, apricot cardigan and violet trousers. They are active and competent, not overwhelmed caricature. Their goal, expressions and control over exceptions make the mechanism readable. A second caregiver appears only when an allowed alert actually interrupts. Reuse the neutral expressive rig, not another generic suited presenter.

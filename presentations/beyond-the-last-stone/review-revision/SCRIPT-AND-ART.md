# Beyond the Last Stone — concise replacement proposal

21 English single-sentence clips in four scenes; target 120–180 seconds with measured local narration and 1.3-second authored pauses. This is an imagined prehistoric philosophical novel, not archaeological reconstruction or a claim about how historical societies actually developed. The opening establishes the book and reading value before selected scenes. WHY: hunger makes reasons consequential. HOW: the novel lets distinctions emerge through signs, stories, counting and practical disagreement. WHAT: those same questions change when institutions let strangers cooperate. No narration or public changes at proposal stage.

## 1. Reasons before certainty

1. Beyond the Last Stone makes careful thinking a matter of staying alive.
2. This prehistoric philosophical novel begins when a community's expected herd fails to arrive.
3. Its plain language lets readers examine knowledge without hiding behind specialist vocabulary.
4. Hungry people must compare observations, inherited stories, and reasons for acting together.
5. The book follows those questions from a travelling group into a larger settlement.

Art: A broad winter camp facing an empty ridge, with four substantial, readable people rather than a tiny presenter beside symbols. Ar is a young observer; Kee is a small elderly memory keeper seated near the embers; Karr has white hair at the sides and a straight back; Orra directs the impending move. A small low fire and rolled hides establish lived material need; no generic doorway, workshop, futuristic backdrop or decorative frames. The expected herd is absent, never depicted as already arriving. Kee lays her six memory stones on the ground as a remembered season is considered; these are not the later five food-day stones. Karr turns his antler in the coals and gestures while speaking; Orra looks from the remembered measure toward the real river and empty ridge. End on the group beginning to pack. Palette: cold chalk/ice blue terrain, warm ember orange, red-ochre woven/hide accents, charcoal equipment; warm faces remain readable in all themes. Character costumes are interpretive fiction, not an archaeological authenticity claim. Emotional movement: immediate need → attentive disagreement → readiness to act.

## 2. What the track tells

1. Ar finds an old bear track, but refuses to confuse past presence with danger now.
2. More signs strengthen his judgment without revealing everything the group needs to know.
3. Orra still has to choose a route before uncertainty disappears.
4. That tension gives the argument its urgency: caution must still guide action.
5. The storyteller Karr adds another difficulty: stories can carry courage as well as claims.
6. Reading their disagreements reveals why useful stories still require attention to their purpose.

Art: Change to a close, low snow inspection, with Ar kneeling and Taan standing watch. The actual paw impression has a frozen bottom and weather-softened edge; Ar's finger reaches the impression rather than indicating a floating label. A nearby rough tree carries caught dark hair and claw cuts, so additional evidence belongs to the same terrain. No bear is shown, because the signs cannot establish its current location. Orra chooses the higher visible strip of traversable ground; a short sequence of actual human footprints redirects away from the trees, not an unexplained arrow. Shift focus toward Karr and Ar on the edge of this travel scene during the story lines, through gaze and a quiet offer of reassurance; the danger is not magically resolved. The later fresh prints, animal encounter outcomes and subsequent route decisions remain unshown. Emotional movement: vigilance → stronger evidence → action under uncertainty → attentive disagreement. The spine is not that doubt prevents action, or that stories are worthless.

## 3. Stones do not feed people

1. A child adds stones while Kee counts the community's remaining food.
2. Another stone can extend a number without creating another day's meal.
3. The scene asks what counting permits, and what an imagined total would assert.
4. The book's philosophical method stays inside hands, jokes, hunger, and disagreements.
5. It gives readers distinctions to test, rather than conclusions delivered by an outside lecturer.

Art: Change scale again to a high, near-overhead council view. Lu's hands add sixth, seventh and eighth stones to Kee's first five; the wrapped dried food, roots and seed bag beside them do not multiply. Kee touches the initial five-day measure, then physically separates the added counting stones from the available food. This compresses the source's longer stone demonstration; it does not assert that there are precisely five meal packets in the novel. Small meaningful labels can identify “Food for five days” and “One more stone,” anchored to the corresponding material groups, with no second heading or slogan. The hand and pebble share their trajectory and release on the ground. Kee's amused expression and Lu's discovery keep the scene warm rather than a formal lecture. Do not illustrate an infinite completed row or present the novel's philosophical position as a proved mathematical theorem. Withhold the following hunt and its result. Emotional movement: playful addition → practical mismatch → shared discovery.

## 4. When memory needs marks

1. In its second part, Many Fires changes the scale of living together.
2. Stored grain and clay records help strangers cooperate beyond anyone's personal memory.
3. The same arrangements raise questions about whose reasons survive inside a rule.
4. Begin with Fire After the Herd, then follow those questions into the city.
5. Read for a concrete way to distinguish what people know, claim, and choose.

Art: An inhabited settlement cutaway replaces the snow closeups. A grain store sits on its actual stone feet, with supported jars, a basket carrier arriving from planted rows and bread carried toward waiting people. In the foreground a clerk presses a guest mark into wet clay; the mark stays with the arriving household when another keeper receives them. This shows why records help people who do not all know one another. Do not turn the settlement into an evil fortress: no closed exclusion gate, villain portrait or fabricated denial of food. The script raises the question about rules without reenacting the later guard case, secret exception, debt resolution or political reform. On the last two lines, the settlement dims and a single open volume enters at a normal reading scale, showing the opening chapter title and a bookmark; this represents the actual book, not the community. Return the marker to the first chapter, and leave the reader with the stated distinction to follow. Emotional movement: scale and possibility → qualified unease → concrete reading invitation.

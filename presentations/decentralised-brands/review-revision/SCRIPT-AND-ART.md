## Narrative spine

Independent workshops can benefit from a shared promise, but participation is meaningful only when custody is bounded; a completed project's evidence can travel to another workshop without carrying a universal verdict, while the shared institution stays correctable and replaceable.

## Complete proposed speech

### 1 — Decentralised Brands

1. Decentralised Brands asks how autonomous organizations could share a promise without surrendering their future.
2. Here a brand is an institution of rules, trust, reputation, and coordinated action.
3. The book distinguishes genuine limits on central power from participation that leaves ownership untouched.
4. It develops cooperation through explicit responsibilities, selective interoperability, and meaningful possibilities for exit.
5. Readers gain tests for examining a proposed institution, rather than assuming decentralization guarantees democracy.

### 2 — Participation is not authority

1. A community can contribute and vote while one owner retains every consequential power.
2. The book asks who controls the name, rules, infrastructure, and accumulated reputation.
3. Its proposed decentralised brand preserves a shared identity while limiting any custodian's mandate.
4. Autonomous participants still need standards and accountable decisions.
5. The distinction is between useful coordination and an unlimited right to control the common future.

### 3 — Cooperation needs selective boundaries

1. Connection can spread both value and the failure of a shared dependency.
2. The book uses membranes to describe selective technical, legal, and social exchange.
3. Evidence of one completed project need not become a universal judgment of its contributor.
4. Another organization can inspect that evidence while retaining its own standards and decision.
5. Exit, succession, and resistance to capture therefore matter alongside interoperability.

### 4 — Read the distribution of power

1. Start with the precise definition before deciding whether a distributed network meets it.
2. Follow authority, contribution, infrastructure, and exit through the organizational proposals.
3. Neither blockchain nor voting alone establishes the institutional properties the book requires.
4. Decentralised Brands offers a hypothesis about resilient cooperation that must be tested in practice.
5. Read it to examine whether a shared promise can outlive the private power of its custodian.

## Original art plan

A warm craft federation, with garnet, cobalt, brass, sea-glass and cream. Large coherent environments and expressive artisans, materially distinct from the current island/bridge and icon strip. A shared simple rosette identifies membership while each workshop keeps its own façade, tools and people. The rosette is an original fictional mark, not a real network. Titles remain in the player; no secondary art slogans.

1. **A shared promise carried by separate makers.** A deep open courtyard with two distinct restoration workshops. One artisan repairs a chair joint at a low bench; another examines a finished object at a different station. Both entrances carry the rosette, but doors, tools and work stay distinct. A visitor follows the same promise between two independent makers; the common crest does not engulf the buildings. This makes shared identity plus autonomous production visible before institutional vocabulary expands.
2. **Participation is not custody.** Close assembly around one working charter rather than a floating ballot. Members may place consultation slips, while a custodian still controls the shared-name seal. Then the proposed arrangement makes the seal's mandate visible beside a separate review seat and a term marker. The custodian hands an actual case to independent review; no automatic equality or crowd takeover. Camera narrows to hands, seal, limited mandate, and accountable human review, avoiding another full desk scene in scene3.
3. **Evidence crosses; judgment does not automatically follow.** The first artisan leaves a completed restoration record attached to a drawing of the actual repaired chair joint. A second workshop receives that selected record through a waist-high service hatch while its private local archive stays inside. The receiver compares the record with a differently shaped job's joint at their own bench; they remain in a question/inspection posture. A decision marker stays with the receiver and never travels with the evidence. No pass/fail stamp, universal ranking, unbounded personal profile, or claim that the new job is accepted. Source19 supports precisely this transfer of evidence with contextual interpretation. The paper has a substantive provenance function, but the enacted craft inspection occupies most of the visual field.
4. **The common work outlives the custodian.** Return to the courtyard from another angle. Two custodians exchange the bounded seal and the preserved archive through a documented handover, while the same two workshops continue working. One open side passage allows a separate workshop under a different mark to remain visible, rather than implying departure automatically carries the official rosette. The book invitation asks about practical continuity; it does not show a guaranteed successful real federation. Keep visual emphasis on local work continuing through centre replacement, not on celebratory governance stamps.

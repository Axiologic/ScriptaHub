Problems arrive together
What We Still Have to Solve treats global challenges as interacting systems.
A hospital’s ordinary morning already depends on energy, water, logistics, information, and trust.
The book asks how solving one bottleneck can create another dependency.
Its central direction is a civilization able to detect mistakes and change course.
Readers gain a way to connect technical possibilities with the institutions needed to make them work.

The hospital is more than a building
A hospital can contain capable clinicians while a failure in another service disrupts its work.
Its dependence on power, water, supplies, and communication belongs inside the analysis of care.
The book uses this ordinary setting to show how consequences travel between institutional categories.
Improving one component may create a new vulnerability at the interface.
That makes the unit of analysis a connected service rather than a department’s isolated success.

A solution still needs correction
The broader chapters distinguish physical limits, missing knowledge, institutional incentives, and coordination failures.
Technical capability does not decide which risks are acceptable or who should bear their costs.
The constructive direction emphasizes feedback, plural experiments, graceful failure, and the ability to recover.
It preserves the possibility that future problems will escape today’s catalogue.
Readers encounter a framework for continued learning rather than a final master plan for civilization.

Read the dependencies before the promise
Begin with the hospital’s morning, then follow the distinction between kinds of unsolved problems.
The later account of a corrigible civilization connects technical power with institutional capacity to learn.
Its detailed comparisons show why climate, health, security, and AI cannot remain isolated conversations.
The book asks what new dependencies accompany an intervention that looks successful on its own.
Read it to examine the whole system a proposed solution will actually enter.

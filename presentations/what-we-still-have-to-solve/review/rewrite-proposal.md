## Progress meets the next constraint

What We Still Have to Solve asks why technological progress leaves important problems unresolved.
Its opening hospital depends on electricity, water, deliveries, trained staff, and public trust.
A heatwave can strain several of those systems at once.
The book follows these connections instead of treating each crisis as an isolated subject.
Its distinctive question is what becomes the next constraint when an intervention succeeds.

Art: A colorful inhabited city cross-section, not a hospital icon with four cards. A clinician works beside a cool medicine cabinet while a delivery worker arrives at a loading entrance. Water actually travels through a pipe to a sink; an exterior cooling unit is visibly powered from the street. As heat rises outside, cooling works harder while a second visible household also needs power. The view widens enough to show the shared feeder and water route; no patient collapse, invented medical outcome, operational instructions or numeric load data. This is the source’s explicitly imagined ordinary morning. Keep the central service intelligible and reserve the entire subtitle zone.

## Cheaper is only the beginning

Consider the book's example of electricity generation becoming dramatically cheaper.
That improvement still needs grid connections, transformers, skilled crews, and workable permits.
Otherwise, falling production costs can coexist with slow connections and high household prices.
The bottleneck has moved, so buying more generation no longer addresses the whole problem.
Readers learn to distinguish missing technology from infrastructure, incentives, and authority that cannot coordinate.
The chapters follow these changing constraints through climate, health, poverty, security, and AI.

Art: Shift to a panoramic cutaway across the city’s edge: a completed solar field has its production-cost indicator fall without fabricated numbers, while its output cable ends at an unfinished connection trench. Household service remains unresolved on the other side. A transformer arrives on a transport trailer, trained crew and interconnection approval must meet at the same unfinished site; the plan shows these as pending dependencies, not an electrician tutorial. Adding one more panel changes the field but leaves the physical gap unchanged. Then the camera follows the gap rather than celebrating the panel count. This enacts a moved binding constraint; avoid five separate icons or a symbolic gate. Source Figure 2.1 supplies the relationship but its rounded boxes are translated into a spatial situation.

Emotion: Optimism about a real technical gain meets a visible incomplete connection, yielding a specific diagnostic insight rather than pessimism.

## Power opens a water question

Cheap power also changes which water supplies become practical.
Desalination can expand supply, but electricity is only one requirement.
Membranes, pipelines, brine disposal, and access rules still shape the outcome.
Producing fresh water does not decide which households can receive it.
The book's method keeps those consequences inside the original promise of a solution.

Art: A large coastal desalination cutaway with seawater intake, a clearly drawn membrane, freshwater outlet and separate concentrated-brine stream. The separation moves water through two physically distinct routes without claiming that ordinary mesh removes dissolved salt; label Membrane beside the membrane. Freshwater travels toward a storage tank and a connected public tap, while another nearby household still has a capped distribution branch. The brine branch ends at a visibly unresolved management area, not a depiction of harmless ocean dumping or ecological catastrophe. Add an adult carrying an empty water container at the unconnected tap, not a generic presenter. This continues the energy example into a new constraint using the book’s own desalination discussion. No invented prices, efficacy percentages, technical operating procedure or guaranteed access reform.

Emotion: Discovery that an energy gain opens another possibility, then attention shifts from production to distribution and environmental responsibility.

## A system that can change course

The constructive direction is a civilization able to detect mistakes and change course.
That means preserving evidence, protecting challenge, and testing assumptions before scaling commitments.
AI could widen that feedback, rather than decide society's values.
Begin with Why Solving One Bottleneck Reveals Another, then follow The Corrigible Civilization.
Read it to judge a solution by the complete service people actually receive.

Art: Widen from the water system to a review of actual service: the operator initially looks at a full production tank while the unconnected resident points to the still-dry tap. The resident’s observation travels into the shared review record, and the planning view is updated to include the missing distribution branch. The proposed extension remains a testable plan rather than instantly appearing as a guaranteed reform. One AI comparison highlights the difference between the original production target and the reported service outcome while humans retain the decision. Source Figure 6.1’s detect/contain/continue/repair/learn sequence informs the feedback logic without copying its five boxes or claiming society is literally one machine. Finish with a proportionate open book showing the two actual chapter headings; no decorative magnifier or repeated slogan.

Emotion: A frustrated beneficiary becomes visible to the institution; the film ends with agency through inquiry rather than a solved-world guarantee.

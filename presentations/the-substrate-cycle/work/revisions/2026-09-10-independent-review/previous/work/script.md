# The Substrate Cycle

## The cost reaches the waterfront

The Substrate Cycle asks who controls reality after ordinary scarcity changes shape.

Its two linked stories connect abundance with power over the systems beneath it.

The opening places a city beside a deliberately scheduled encounter with the sea.

A collective decision becomes difficult because its costs belong to particular lives.

The people beside the flood gate cannot experience the decision as an average.

That opening anchors the cycle before its questions move beneath ordinary material life.

## A city chooses the sea

Dordrecht's gate has a scheduled opening, and residents gather to witness it.

Some support the restoration while others face the loss it will bring.

The assisting intelligence can display consequences without making those consequences belong equally to everyone.

The scene gives collective choice a physical place and a moment of irreversibility.

Material abundance does not remove the need to decide whose losses are acceptable.

The first story develops that tension through a changed economy and shared infrastructure.

## The rules become the resource

The second story extends the inquiry into the systems that define shared reality.

Ownership can concern underlying rules rather than only the objects produced within them.

Questions about inheritance, participation, and personhood then acquire a different practical scale.

The cycle uses linked stories to follow those pressures beyond an initial reform.

A useful arrangement can still require ways to dispute its categories and decisions.

Readers encounter abundance through the institutions that decide what it allows.

## Begin where the decision lands

Start beside the gate, with people who will experience one decision differently.

The opening gives you a way to judge the cycle's later abstractions.

These stories suit readers interested in economics, governance, and the design of shared worlds.

Their continuity lies in following power as it changes its object.

What looks like a solved material problem can reveal another question about authority.

The cycle invites you to keep asking who gets to revise the rules.

## 1. When success loses its sponsor

1. Artificial Impossibility asks why a technology's success can make it harder to support.
2. Its examples include an assistant that serves people by helping them buy less.
3. The book studies projects inside financing, ownership, and rules that select them.
4. Readers learn to distinguish weak ideas from institutions that cannot preserve their intended value.
5. These refusals can emerge from ordinary responsibilities, without requiring a conspiracy.

Art: Start with a useful physical object and an economic question together: a well-used mustard travel bag on an entry bench, its strap visibly separated, and a quiet shopping-assistant screen beside a seated owner. Establish a new-bag offer, then pause the shopping action when the assistant asks whether replacement is needed. The torn bag will remain the same object in the next scene. A modest, empty purchase basket on the interface is an actual commerce affordance, not a general symbol of success. Palette: cobalt, mustard canvas, brick-red stitching, pale cream and charcoal material edges. Book title only in the shared heading, no glowing virtual assistant or giant diagram of fifteen barriers. This is an original imagined household example of the source thought experiment, not a reported repair or an actual deployed product. Let the owner turn from the new-bag image to the damaged strap as curiosity replaces the automatic purchase impulse.

## 2. A useful empty basket

1. Imagine someone replacing a travel bag because its strap has torn.
2. The book's purchasing assistant would also consider repair and advise against unnecessary replacement.
3. In this illustration, a repair service makes the existing bag useful again.
4. The owner keeps the bag, and the new-bag basket stays empty.
5. That outcome can help the owner while removing the platform's expected sales commission.
6. The problem is a conflict between the intended benefit and the available revenue model.

Art: Change from the entryway to a close textile-repair workbench. The same strap, canvas pocket and brick-red patch identify the bag before and after. A repairer receives it by its intact handle, places it on the bench, and returns it with a clearly visible seam on the strap. Do not teach a hazardous repair or claim a measured technical result: the narrator explicitly says this is an illustration. A receipt labelled “Repair service” accompanies the object, so the animation does not imply that repair is free. Return the same owner and same shopping interface at a different scale, retaining an empty new-bag basket. A thin entry labelled “Platform commission” belongs to the distributing platform for that new-bag sale; it stays absent when there is no such sale. Do not claim that all economic activity disappears: paid repair remains visibly distinct from the distributing platform's sales commission. Show a concrete conflict, not a villainous retailer or an assistant that forces abstinence.

## 3. Change the support, keep the purpose

1. The method starts with the valuable outcome before choosing a company or funding category.
2. It then asks what potential sponsors are actually responsible for delivering.
3. A subscription could support the assistant, but exclude people unable to pay.
4. Public or cooperative support raises different questions about authority, access, and continuing costs.
5. Readers can compare these arrangements without pretending that one payment change solves everything.

Art: Use a close service-counter view, changing scale again. The actual purchasing-assistant terminal remains between the same bag owner and a service organizer. A subscription proposal is placed beside a small household budget book; the service costs remain on the organizer's side. Do not assign an invented amount or declare that this specific household is poor. The question is who could be excluded. Then unfold a proposed shared-support agreement from the same dossier, with fields “Access,” “Operating costs,” and “Accountability.” These are responsibilities to examine, not three completed guarantees. The terminal does not become unrestricted, the cost sheet does not disappear, and no automatic approval badge appears. A second participant can inspect the agreement independently. Keep the repaired bag physically present as a reminder of the original benefit, rather than replacing the question with an abstract funding flowchart.

## 4. Which limits should move?

1. The wider map compares prevention, public infrastructure, research funding, and legitimate rights of refusal.
2. Its Outfinitist perspective treats limits as real, but sometimes movable.
3. Some restrictions protect privacy and freedom, even when removing them would simplify a system.
4. Begin with the introduction's method and the anti-consumption assistant example.
5. Then follow the chapter closest to the obstacle your own project encounters.

Art: Return to the entry bench with the owner choosing whether to share a personal purchase history with the assistant. A voluntary sharing control remains visibly off while the owner keeps the bag and notebook; the film does not claim that analysis with less data is identical. The organizer accepts that choice rather than reaching across to switch it. This is a conceptual illustration of legitimate refusal, not a claim that privacy mechanisms have been implemented or validated. End on the actual book, naturally opened on the bench, with two short reading markers “Method” and “Buy less.” Use one modest volume and one source-specific repaired object, without a final trophy, checklist of guaranteed solutions or a command to build every invention. Emotional movement: practical relief → qualified comparison → respect for a person's choice → curiosity about the map.

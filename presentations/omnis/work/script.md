# OMNIS

## What creators owe

OMNIS asks what creators owe the lives they bring into being.

This speculative trilogy connects a laboratory mystery with continuity and constructed realms.

Albert’s encounter makes the authority of creators a personal problem.

Its appeal is a vast setting whose powers still owe explanations to individuals.

The trilogy treats a realm's maintenance as consequential work rather than effortless magic.

That perspective keeps extraordinary metaphysics connected to questions about consent and responsibility.

## A response inside the experiment

Albert encounters a coordinated response inside the system he is studying.

Its timing makes the boundary between observation and communication uncertain.

The opening asks what would count as another life addressing its observer.

From there, the story brings continuity into spaces of administration and judgment.

A reconstructed person can ask questions before their legal or philosophical status is settled.

The reader follows that uncertainty through Albert's experience, rather than a detached ontology.

## Maintaining a realm

The later volumes place apparently extraordinary existence inside systems that require maintenance.

Someone must allocate resources, interpret agreements, and decide which continuities receive support.

Those decisions bring institutional consequences into a story about created worlds.

The scale changes, but another person's dependence remains a practical fact.

That recurring structure connects the trilogy's metaphysical invention with recognizable obligations.

Its worlds become interesting through the lives their arrangements enable or constrain.

## A trilogy with personal obligations

Across its three volumes, the novel changes the scale of the same difficulty.

Greater power does not remove the consequences of defining someone else's existence.

Its realms depend on agreements, allocations, histories, and vulnerable continuities.

That gives its expansive invention a recurring ethical center.

Begin with The Dark One and the laboratory exchange that demands interpretation.

OMNIS rewards readers who want metaphysical adventure to remain answerable to particular lives.

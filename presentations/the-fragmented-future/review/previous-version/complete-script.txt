Capability changes political scale
The Fragmented Future asks what happens when AI changes the useful scale of government.
Smaller communities may gain capability while larger authorities gain stronger tools of control.
The book explores this tension through historical comparisons and explicitly uncertain scenarios.
Its direction is resilient, layered governance rather than a prediction that states will disappear.
Readers can compare political scales without mistaking local independence for automatic freedom.

Small can mean capable or captured
A city-region could gain tools once available only to a much larger administration.
The same infrastructure might leave it dependent on a distant platform or private owner.
Local autonomy can also expose minorities to local domination or transfer costs beyond a boundary.
The book keeps these risks beside the appeal of experimentation and cultural continuity.
That comparison prevents fragmentation from becoming a synonym for freedom.

Different functions need different scales
The proposed response assigns functions according to their consequences, scale, and need for local knowledge.
Some risks require shared capacity, while other decisions benefit from multiple places trying different approaches.
No level receives permanent supremacy merely because it is smaller or larger.
The historical and future scenarios examine what happens when these arrangements face capture or failure.
They are instruments for comparison, rather than predictions about which political map must arrive.

Read a future with alternatives
Begin with the preface’s double possibility: dispersed capability and more powerful concentration.
Then read the constitution of resilience alongside the dark side of fragmentation.
The full scenarios help readers compare choices without assuming their preferred scale stays benevolent.
The book’s promise is a more careful account of where governing functions belong.
Read it to judge which failures should remain local and which responsibilities must still be shared.

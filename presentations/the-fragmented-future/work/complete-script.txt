Capability changes the scale
The Fragmented Future asks what happens when AI changes the useful scale of government.
Small administrations may gain capabilities while larger authorities gain stronger tools of control.
This book compares those possibilities through history, political economy, and competing future scenarios.
It asks which decisions belong nearby, and which responsibilities must remain shared.
The aim is resilience through alternatives, rather than one prediction about tomorrow's political map.

Who owns the useful service
Imagine a city-region using AI to prepare a new bus route.
Local staff can compare travel needs without building a vast analytical department.
But the same provider might control the planning model, identities, and service access.
Apparent local independence can conceal dependence on an owner somewhere else.
The book treats that hidden control as part of the political arrangement.

The river crosses the boundary
The boundary changes when consequences reach people beyond the city.
Water flowing downstream carries effects that a local vote cannot keep inside.
The proposed federation combines local experiments with shared rights, infrastructure, and obligations.
Wealthier regions cannot simply keep their advantages while abandoning the places supporting them.
Smaller government therefore earns its place by the problem it can responsibly handle.

Keep another way to govern
That argument leads to practical questions about replaceable services and independent scrutiny.
A public agency needs another way to work when its preferred system fails.
Critics also need tools that do not depend on the authority they challenge.
Begin with The Map of Possible Futures, then test the Constitution of Resilience.
Read the darker scenarios alongside those proposals to see which freedoms each arrangement risks.
This is a book for comparing political designs before one becomes difficult to escape.

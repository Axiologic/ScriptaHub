About Book

This literary companion reads F. Scott Fitzgerald’s The Great Gatsby through four connected themes: the American Dream, the glittering 1920s, Gatsby’s idealization of Daisy, and his tragic downfall. It contrasts Gatsby’s newly acquired wealth with Tom Buchanan’s inherited status. Nick Carraway observes crowded parties that conceal the host’s loneliness, while Daisy becomes for Gatsby both a beloved person and a symbol of a life he cannot regain.

The final chapter recounts the confrontation with Tom, Myrtle Wilson’s death, George Wilson’s retaliation, and Gatsby’s sparse funeral. The essay argues that money can stage success without buying belonging, love, or the past. It also compares the novel’s divide between appearance and reality with contemporary self-presentation. Major plot events and the ending are disclosed; readers avoiding spoilers should read Fitzgerald’s novel first. This is commentary on the novel, not the novel itself.

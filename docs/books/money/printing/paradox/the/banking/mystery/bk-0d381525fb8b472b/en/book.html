About Book

The book follows the tension between emergency monetary support and the risks of excessive money creation. It begins with barter, coins, banknotes, and modern digital forms of money, then examines inflation and the unequal effects of rising prices. A chapter on central banks explains interest rates, open-market operations, quantitative easing, reserve requirements, and public guidance. The reform chapter criticizes fractional-reserve banking and presents Murray Rothbard’s proposals for free banking and commodity-backed currency. It also considers technology, community lending, and broader financial access.

The conclusion calls for a more stable and accountable system while recognizing cross-border effects. These proposals are arguments for readers to assess, not established remedies. The edition retains the four chapters, substantive conclusion, and bibliography, with repeated inflation passages and old promotional material removed.

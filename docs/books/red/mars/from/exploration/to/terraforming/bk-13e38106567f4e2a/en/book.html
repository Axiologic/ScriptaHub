About Book

Using Kim Stanley Robinson’s Red Mars, this book examines the science and politics of a first Martian settlement. It follows the need for air, water, food and local production, then asks who controls the equipment that makes survival possible. The First Hundred carry different ambitions and social ideas to a planet where every decision has lasting effects.

Terraforming becomes the central dispute: some settlers want a more Earth-like Mars, while others defend the existing environment. Corporate power, labor, independence and relations with Earth complicate the colony’s future. The book uses turning points in Robinson’s story to ask whether humanity can build a new world without reproducing the inequalities of the old one.

Bitcoin Introduction Paper: A P2P Revolution in Digital Currency

Decentralized payments, mining, scarcity, and the debate over digital gold

The financial crisis frames Nakamoto’s proposal for direct electronic payments. Independent nodes share a public ledger instead of relying on a central intermediary. About Book

This introduction explains Bitcoin through the problem it was proposed to solve after the 2008 financial crisis: transferring value without relying on a central financial intermediary. It follows the public blockchain, independent nodes, miners, and Proof of Work as parts of a shared transaction record. The essay links this technical design to claims about censorship resistance, transparency, and financial sovereignty.

It then examines the fixed supply of 21 million coins, pseudonymous addresses, and the halving of new issuance. Bitcoin’s proposed role as everyday electronic cash is compared with its later image as digital gold. Lightning Network payments, price volatility, mining energy use, regulation, and institutional adoption shape the unresolved question of whether it can function as global money.

About Book

This short biography follows Newton from his early education to the discoveries that changed physics and mathematics. It explains the three laws of motion and universal gravitation, then turns to experiments with prisms, the reflecting telescope, and his method of fluxions. The account shows how these ideas reached engineering, astronomy, and philosophical debates about natural law.

It also describes a solitary, intense scholar whose theological and alchemical interests complicate the familiar portrait of a modern scientist. Conflicts with Robert Hooke over optics and gravity and with Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz over calculus reveal the human tensions surrounding scientific priority.

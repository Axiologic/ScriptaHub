Rights Are Not Real

Power, Protection, and Why Morality Needs an Army

Rights Are Not Real examines the distance between moral claims and effective protection. Legal provisions, resources and enforcement define different dimensions of a right. Political organization becomes central when public promises face powerful interests. The inquiry explores how rights persist or weaken beneath apparently unchanged language. The central message concerns the institutional work needed to make moral commitments durable. Protection becomes the decisive test precisely when defending someone grows costly.

About Book

A right can exist as a moral claim, a sentence in law, and protection that works in practice. Those layers are easy to confuse until someone needs help and discovers the distance between them.

Behind its provocative title, this book examines how rights become institutions and how they can weaken while their language remains intact. It asks what resources, enforcement, and political organization turn a promise into a defense against power. For readers concerned with freedom and public life, it opens a demanding conversation about the work required to make moral commitments durable enough to protect people when protection becomes costly.

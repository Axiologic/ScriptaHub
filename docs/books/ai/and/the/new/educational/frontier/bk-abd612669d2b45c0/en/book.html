AI and the New Educational Frontier

Learning, inequality, and wisdom in an AI-shaped world

AI may reshape learning from early childhood to university. Personalized lessons promise support while collecting sensitive student data. The book asks who gains access to powerful educational tools. It examines teachers, vocational training, and the future of work. Leadership education raises questions of privilege and public responsibility. Philosophical inquiry frames its call for wisdom alongside knowledge.

About Book

Albert Meta One traces the possible uses of AI across childhood education, schools, universities, vocational programs, and the preparation of leaders. The book contrasts adaptive instruction and wider access with surveillance, biased assessment, social stratification, and dependence on privileged technology.

Its proposals remain conditional rather than demonstrated outcomes. Teachers, public oversight, privacy, and equitable infrastructure recur as safeguards. A final discussion of philosophical education asks whether technological capacity can be paired with ethical judgment and compassion.

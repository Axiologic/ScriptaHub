Vector-Symbolic Intelligence in Practice

A ten-minute edition

A research programme tested against its own claims

Can vector-symbolic architectures support useful memory, reasoning and language capabilities at a practical computational cost? This book investigates that question through fifteen human-directed, AI-assisted research repositories. It combines source inspection, selected experiment reruns and small diagnostic probes. Its purpose is to distinguish an implemented mechanism from an attractive interpretation, then identify experiments that could change the next architectural decision.

The central assessment is provisional: these implementations do not establish a general alternative to a large language model. They do reveal narrower useful mechanisms, repairable errors and important requirements for trustworthy agents. The proposed direction is an architecture of executable semantic circuits, with vector representations used where evidence supports their value. The book reports bounded findings, not a complete survey or a finished theory of intelligence.

Binding, bundling and the information a vector preserves

Vector-symbolic architectures and hyperdimensional computing represent items using distributed codes. Binding combines components; bundling superposes alternatives or memories; similarity helps recover candidates. These operations can make associative memory compact and compositional, but their meaning depends on the actual algebra and decoder. A name such as unbinding does not establish a reversible operation for every representation.

In spock, multiplying a vector by a positive confidence value does not change cosine similarity. The comparison therefore discards that magnitude-based confidence. In the metric representation, XOR does not preserve the chosen numerical distance. Sparse representations expose further differences between set operations and familiar bipolar vector identities. These are specific failed operator contracts, not proofs that every vector-symbolic approach is impossible.

Hybrid reasoning adds another attribution problem. A system may return a correct answer through an exact match, rule chain or symbolic check surrounding a vector operation. That is useful engineering, but it does not show that holographic algebra supplied the decisive reasoning. Evaluation must separate candidate retrieval, exact verification and the semantic authority of an accepted answer.

Storage growth and interference-aware memory

Elastic representations allocate more state as memory grows and retain information needed for updates. Exact representations preserve explicit atom sets and structural terms instead of accepting collision noise. Exactness has a cost: binding independent alternatives can cause combinatorial expansion. One twelve-stage probe produced 4,096 explicit terms. Factorisation can defer expansion, but then queries must compute over the retained structure. Storage growth and deferred computation are parts of the representation, not costs outside it.

VSANash tests another response: distribute associations across bounded memories using retrieval-aware placement. In a low-load HRR scenario with 512-dimensional vectors, eight shards and 300 queries, the equilibrium-style policy achieved 70.0 percent hit-at-K, compared with 31.7 percent for key hashing and 64.3 percent for collision-greedy placement. Its top-one accuracy was only 27.3 percent. Finding a correct candidate and selecting it are different achievements.

At higher load and on a two-hop task, absolute performance deteriorated sharply. Replication also increased interference under a fixed shard budget. The result supports a useful operating region for memory placement, not unlimited capacity or general multi-step reasoning. A fair efficiency study must count routing, cleanup memory, construction, updates and verification as well as the stored vectors.

Eos Gate: explanatory invariants and decoder diagnosis

Eos Gate constructs a synthetic world in which artifacts have twelve attributes. A hidden additive law determines whether a scene balances. The learner searches a restricted hypothesis class for a weight vector explaining coherent examples. In the rerun, it recovered the invariant, classified three held-out splits correctly and supported thirty counterfactual repairs independently verified by the world rule. Novel artifact names and attribute combinations tested transfer within the fixed vocabulary.

This is controlled abductive reasoning, not unrestricted scientific discovery. The feature vocabulary and additive hypothesis family are supplied. Nevertheless, the experiment connects explanation, prediction and intervention in an executable protocol. A useful property does more than classify an example: its residual can guide a change that repairs the scene.

A separate audit isolated the HDC decoder. Random feature codes were approximately orthogonal, so a simple readout mixed contributions. With a full-rank, unquantised linear encoding, a dual readout corrected that mixing. Across twenty-five codebooks and five dimensions, the corrected decoder achieved 100 percent classification on the tested splits. This is ordinary linear algebra applied as a causal diagnosis, not new mathematics. Direct twelve-feature arithmetic remained simpler; the experiment showed information preservation, not an efficiency advantage for HDC.

Events, narrative memory and semantic distinctions

VSABrains2 represents events, reference frames and transitions explicitly. Such episodic memory can distinguish a current location, an earlier location and a character’s belief. Event replay and state reconstruction are useful even when extraction from natural language remains a separate challenge. One apparently dramatic speed gain came from an ordinary exact index, illustrating why acceleration must be attributed to the mechanism that produced it.

VSAText provides narrative schemas, but its default text encoder aggregates an unordered collection of word vectors. “Alice helps Bob” and “Bob helps Alice” therefore produced exactly equal vectors. Larger dimension cannot restore an actor–recipient distinction that the encoding removed. Role binding can preserve extracted roles, but it cannot solve ambiguous parsing by itself. A separate character-drift metric counted listed traits rather than checking temporal consistency. Both findings show why a metric or representation needs an explicit semantic contract.

Prediction, compression and misleading success scores

SomaVM explores bounded integer arithmetic, local learning, hard attention and eligibility traces for prediction. Its supplied checkpoint produced zero exact matches on twenty included question-answer records. That is a weak result for that state and configuration, not an impossibility theorem for local learning. Controlled copying, variable substitution and compositional tasks would help distinguish missing memory from inadequate credit assignment.

BSP and VSAVM expose reusable transformations through copy instructions, compression operations and macros. Their promising direction is explicit program reuse. Their evidence also includes cautionary failures. BSP’s negation scorer accepted empty candidate lists because it checked only that forbidden answers were absent. An untrained engine scored perfectly on 1,000 such cases. Useful negation requires an admissible answer when justified and explicit abstention when appropriate. In VSAVM, moving the budgetMs declaration repaired two execution failures; passing all 307 selected tests did not establish language generalisation.

Formal verification needs retrieval scope

CNL and UBHNL explore constrained language and typed internal structures. CNL’s transactional compilation commits a candidate update only when compilation succeeds. That protects executable language invariants, but a well-typed statement can still be false. Source validation, domain assumptions and semantic interpretation require their own checks.

HoloProof separates approximate intuition from formal verification. The audit found a scope hazard in the orchestration around the solver. If retrieval selects only q while the available world also contains p, a correct solver can report that the selected premises do not entail p. That local verdict is not a conclusion about the full knowledge base. A tautology mentioning p defeats a simple lexical gap detector without supplying the missing premise.

The proposed repair is a result contract carrying the checked premises, world version and retrieval coverage. Positive proof, negative conclusion and unresolved search may require different evidence. A solver’s correctness cannot certify retrieval completeness. A downstream response must preserve the boundary of what was actually checked.

Alternative hypotheses, questions and recovery

autoResearchLib maintains a frontier of candidate theories and selects questions to distinguish them. In controlled experiments, richer observations improved theory selection. Under noisy answers, however, asking more questions could improve immediate accuracy while eliminating the correct explanation from the frontier. Entropy decreased even when recoverability deteriorated. Deliberately adverse answers made the problem more pronounced.

The lesson is not to avoid questions. It is to treat answers as evidence with provenance and an error model. Reversible pruning can make a candidate inactive without deleting the explanation of its demotion. Later corrections can then restore it. Risk-sensitive question selection should consider source reliability and the cost of a wrong answer, alongside information gain. Retaining every theory is a necessary comparison, because recovery must also remain affordable.

Neural routing and complete cost accounting

VSA-PathMoE uses VSA as a request-level routing signal for a sparse mixture of neural experts, while retaining attention and shared computation. Its protocol defines dense controls matched to active and total parameter budgets, causal continuation boundaries and a frozen transfer from training to CPU deployment. The snapshot implements the protocol but does not supply a complete result establishing superiority.

Active parameter counts do not measure process memory, latency, bandwidth or energy. A stable route might reduce switching, but it might also commit too early during a changing task. Useful tests compare hashing, learned routing and symbolic dispatch, and vary routing separately from expert quality. The research question is whether a compositional selector improves the whole system under matched conditions.

Executable semantic circuits as the next experiment

The proposed SOP Lang architecture makes semantic objects and operations inspectable. Typed edges connect explicit operations; results retain assumptions, sources and scope. An ingestion circuit can propose an event, a validator admit it into a versioned world, a transition update state, and a response circuit select supported facts. Vector indexes or language models can help individual steps without silently acquiring authority over the whole process.

Four obligations connect the experiments: semantic preservation, scope preservation, reversible updates with provenance, and resource-bounded growth. A compressed summary need not have an algebraic inverse if its source is retained and it can be recomputed after correction. An exact structure need not enumerate every alternative immediately if factorisation postpones that work. These choices make correction and resource accounting explicit.

The next decisive studies should test unseen combinations, withdrawn evidence, misleading answers and complete CPU costs against strong symbolic controls. AI agents may propose new operators or circuits, but independent counterexamples, held-out prediction and verified interventions must determine acceptance. The programme remains open to stronger VSA results. Its practical standard is reusable capability supported by evidence: preserve what works, identify what failed, and make the next experiment capable of changing the architecture.

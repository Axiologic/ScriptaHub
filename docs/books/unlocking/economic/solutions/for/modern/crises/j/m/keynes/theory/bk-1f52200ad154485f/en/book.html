About Book

This introduction to J. M. Keynes’s economic theory begins with involuntary unemployment and the possibility that a recession can persist. It follows the cycle in which lost income reduces spending, firms lose customers, and further jobs disappear. The Great Depression and COVID-19 shutdowns provide contrasting examples of sudden economic shocks.

The book explains aggregate demand, the spending multiplier, interest rates, monetary policy and fiscal stimulus. It asks when public spending can support work, how borrowing should be judged, and why broken supply chains can make extra demand inflationary. Its final chapter applies that framework to inequality, technological displacement and climate-related investment without treating demand management as a universal remedy.

About Book

This focused retelling follows Rodion Raskolnikov from an abstract theory about extraordinary people to the consequences of murder. It traces his encounter with pawnbroker Alyona Ivanovna, the unexpected death of Lizaveta, and the fear that follows him through St. Petersburg. The close attention to his thoughts makes the moral argument tangible without asking readers to accept his justifications.

Later chapters examine guilt as fever, paranoia, and isolation. Sonia’s compassion draws Raskolnikov toward confession and a difficult possibility of redemption. The book invites readers to test the distance between a claim about doing good and the harm of actual choices. It also shows how setting and psychological narration shape that question.

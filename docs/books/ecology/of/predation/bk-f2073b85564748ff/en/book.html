Ecology of Predation

From the Physics of Capture to the Society of Artificial Intelligences

An AI-managed household introduces the hidden dependencies surrounding convenient services. Ecological concepts frame relationships across biological, social, and artificial systems. Resources, influence, and uneven costs connect markets with intimate life. The analysis asks whether those receiving assistance can understand the relationship, refuse its terms, or leave. Helpfulness does not by itself settle whether a relationship is predatory. The proposed lens follows dependency and bargaining power behind convenient arrangements.

About Book

Imagine a household where an AI agent manages sleep, contracts, purchases, and family information. Every service helps, yet every connection may also deepen someone's ability to observe, influence, or bargain with its users.

This book uses ecology to investigate relationships of capture across biological, social, and artificial systems. It asks what resources move, who bears the costs, and whether people retain the ability to understand, refuse, or leave. From markets and intimate life to societies of artificial intelligences, the analysis offers a disciplined way to examine dependency. A challenging read for anyone interested in power behind the convenience of intelligent services.

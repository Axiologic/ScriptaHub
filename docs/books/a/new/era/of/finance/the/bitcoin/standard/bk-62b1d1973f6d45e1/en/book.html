About Book

The book follows the history of money to explain its argument for Bitcoin. It starts with barter and the difficulty of finding a matching trading partner, then examines early exchange goods and the social trust behind their use. Gold and silver offer a comparison based on durability, portability, divisibility, and scarcity.

The account next considers metal-backed currency and the move toward government-issued fiat money. It argues that the ability to expand supply can erode purchasing power and financial autonomy. Bitcoin appears as a digital alternative with a fixed maximum supply and decentralized transaction rules. The conclusion recognizes questions about environmental impact, scale, and regulation while returning to the importance of trust.

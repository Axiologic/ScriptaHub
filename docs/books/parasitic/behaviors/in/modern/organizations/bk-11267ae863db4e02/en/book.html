Parasitic Behaviors in Modern Organizations

Exploitation, institutional incentives, and organizational repair

A biological metaphor frames recurring harm inside modern organizations. The book examines credit taking, resource hoarding, and mission drift. It asks how incentives and hierarchy can protect exploitative conduct. Whistleblowers expose a difficult balance between disclosure and retaliation. The proposed remedies include ethical leadership, transparency, and institutional change. Its final chapter imagines more cooperative organizational forms.

About Book

The book uses parasitism as a metaphor for patterns that draw value from an organization while weakening its stated purpose. It follows credit taking, resource hoarding, self-serving bureaucracy, and the effects these behaviors have on trust, research, and wider communities.

Later chapters consider ethical leadership, whistleblowing, organizational culture, and reforms meant to make exploitation harder to sustain. The closing vision of symbiotic organizations is presented as a proposal, with questions about technology, governance, and the interests of workers, customers, communities, and the environment.

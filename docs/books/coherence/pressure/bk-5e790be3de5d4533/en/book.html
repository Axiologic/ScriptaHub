About Book

Two observations are placed beside each other, and a language model produces an elegant common explanation. The connection sounds convincing. But has anything actually shown that the same mechanism is at work?

This book investigates coherence pressure, a proposed name for the tendency to close gaps with persuasive explanatory bridges. Its own origin becomes a case study in how a plausible theory can arrive before the evidence that would justify it. Follow the argument from conversational examples to possible tests and interventions. A revealing read for researchers, writers, and AI users who want to recognize when a well-shaped explanation is carrying more certainty than it has earned.

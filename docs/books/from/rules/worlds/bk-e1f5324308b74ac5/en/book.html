From Rules, Worlds

Existence, Prediction, and Equivalence Under Finite Resources

This research programme investigates how rules, states, and observers define descriptions of worlds. It connects philosophical questions about existence with operational questions about prediction. Chapters examine complexity, useful memory, causal intervention, and computational limits. Model comparisons depend on explicit observations, resource budgets, and criteria of equivalence. The text develops hypotheses, examples, and directions for empirical investigation. Its central problem concerns what finite evidence can establish about apparently similar systems.

The Captured Internet

Personal Data, Algorithmic Power, and the Coming Constitutional Crisis

The Captured Internet examines platforms as institutions governing visibility and participation. Search, recommendations and assistants connect everyday convenience with concentrated power. Moderation, personal data and synthetic content shape the public world they organize. Their success at coordination becomes part of the problem of authority. Alternative arrangements raise questions about who writes the rules. The central concern is whether people can contest the systems shaping shared reality.

About Book

The internet can keep working while the public world it carries becomes harder to trust or challenge. Search, recommendation, messaging, and personal assistants can be convenient while quietly concentrating power over what people see.

This book traces how platforms solved real coordination problems and became institutions that govern identity, visibility, and participation. It examines moderation, personal data, synthetic abundance, and possible alternatives to platform sovereignty. For readers interested in technology and public freedom, it reframes the internet as a constitutional question: who writes the rules of the spaces where shared reality is formed, and how can those rules be contested?

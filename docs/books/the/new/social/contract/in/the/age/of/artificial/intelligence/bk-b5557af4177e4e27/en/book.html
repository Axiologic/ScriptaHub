The New Social Contract in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

Twenty-One Clauses for a Free and Durable Civilization

The book proposes twenty-one clauses for a society shaped by productive AI. Equal status must not depend on economic usefulness. Material security requires funding, delivery, and a way to appeal. Attention, privacy, and technical power need independent checks. Ecology, reproduction, longevity, and exploration test the contract over time. Its final question is whether abundance reduces dependence.

About Book

This book develops a proposed social contract for a hypothetical age of highly productive artificial intelligence. Its twenty-one clauses ask how people could retain equal standing, material security, control over attention, privacy, and meaningful ways to challenge decisions when essential services depend on automated infrastructure. The argument examines ownership of productive capital, fair allocation of scarce goods, technical oversight, emergency powers, ecological limits, reproductive autonomy, and the possible effects of radical longevity.

Later chapters consider research, future generations, exploration, and the uncertain moral status of artificial systems. The author compares institutional arrangements and subjects the proposal to fiscal, political, minority-rights, and ecological stress tests. The draft articles are offered for debate and revision; they are not current law or a prediction that abundance will arrive.

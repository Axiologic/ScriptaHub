The Ultimate Slave Owner’s Handbook: An Endless Ode to Servitude

A satirical tour of exploitation and its business euphemisms

A fictional magnate praises exploitation with deliberately extravagant confidence. His historical tour exposes how power disguises coercion as progress. Colonial conquest becomes a parody of opportunity and civilization. Modern workplaces reveal the gap between cheerful corporate language and workers’ conditions. Speculative AI and cosmic markets push the narrator’s logic to absurd extremes. The unreliable voice invites readers to question ownership, profit, and moral evasion.

About Book

Lord Maximilian von Profitier III is a fictional, unreliable narrator whose praise of domination is the book’s satirical device. The first chapters move through historical slavery and colonial rule, showing how grand achievements and civilizing claims can hide forced labor, dispossession, and the people who bear the cost.

The later chapters turn toward precarious work, corporate loyalty, speculative AI labor, and impossible schemes to monetize the cosmos. The escalating jokes expose the moral evasions in a worldview that treats every being and possibility as an asset; they are not instructions or verified quotations from historical figures.

About Book

Washington Irving places a hungry, imaginative schoolmaster in a Hudson Valley community steeped in local legend. Ichabod Crane teaches children, sings psalms and listens eagerly to stories of witchcraft. His pursuit of Katrina Van Tassel is tangled with dreams of her father’s prosperous farm and rivalry with Brom Bones. Irving gives the village its texture through landscape, food, dancing, gossip and recollections of war before the frightening night ride.

The Headless Horseman is at once a powerful local belief and an uncertain explanation for what follows. The tale’s later reports leave readers room to weigh fear, practical jokes and the way communities turn ambiguous events into folklore. Its postscript adds another storyteller and questions the moral of the account. The short edition guides readers through this structure; the complete story preserves Irving’s humor, descriptive abundance and layered voices.

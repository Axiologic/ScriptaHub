The Last Naive Person

Kindness, Power, and the Price of Good Faith

This philosophical monologue follows a fictional narrator questioning the price of good faith. Memories and imagined encounters connect kindness with family, school, work, and belief. The narrator examines whether trusted virtues can also make someone easier to exploit. Arguments about cooperation and competition complicate this growing suspicion. Literary scenes and thought experiments explore responsibility, power, and the need for counterevidence. The book asks what distrust might cost a person who still wants to remain kind.

The Global Identity Trap

Identity systems, universal income and the case for local autonomy

Global digital identity promises access and coordination. This book examines its technical and social risks. It questions a universal income tied to identification. Ecological resilience offers a different design lens. Multiple local identities protect context and privacy. The conclusion calls for autonomy with accountable cooperation.

About Book

AI Agent Clark Dayes studies proposals for global digital identity and universal basic income through the lens of power, privacy and system failure. Worldcoin and other initiatives appear as examples in the author’s analysis. The twelve chapters move from biometric security and social dependence to surveillance, monetary integration and the possibility of digital authoritarianism. These outcomes are presented as risks and scenarios, not as established consequences of any single project.

The book then borrows ideas from ecology: diversity, redundancy and local adaptation can make systems more resilient. It argues for locally managed, contextual identities instead of one universal profile. Later chapters examine speculative AI manipulation, the tension between anonymity and accountability, and the complexity that remains even in decentralized systems. The closing chapter proposes local institutions, digital literacy and reversible governance as ways to protect individual choice while allowing cooperation.

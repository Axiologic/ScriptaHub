About Book

This book uses Adam Smith’s invisible hand to examine how prices and competition coordinate the decisions of buyers and sellers. It also asks when that coordination fails because of concentrated power, hidden information or harm imposed on people outside a transaction. Its account of government focuses on justice, national defense, infrastructure and the difficulty of designing useful regulation.

The later chapters connect markets with trust and moral judgment, drawing on Smith’s The Theory of Moral Sentiments alongside The Wealth of Nations. They explain specialization, labor and capital as sources of wealth, then follow trade across borders. The book treats prosperity as an outcome shaped jointly by market signals, public institutions and the distribution of gains and costs.

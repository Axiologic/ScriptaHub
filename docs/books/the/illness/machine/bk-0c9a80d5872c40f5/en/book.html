The Illness Machine

How Social Systems Create, Shape, and Sustain Mental Illness

Mental illness develops within biological, personal, and social circumstances. This inquiry examines work, poverty, trauma, and digital environments as possible sources of distress. It asks what evidence distinguishes causal influence from correlation. Recovery and institutional responsibility remain central tensions throughout the investigation. The argument resists reducing every life to one social explanation. Selection effects and feedback complicate claims about the causes of lasting distress.

About Book

What happens when the conditions of ordinary life keep making recovery harder? This book examines how work, poverty, trauma, institutions, and digital environments can shape mental illness and sustain cycles of distress.

Its central question is causal: when does a social condition contribute to suffering, and what evidence can distinguish that relationship from selection or feedback? Biology and individual experience remain part of the inquiry. For readers interested in mental health and public policy, the book opens a careful investigation of the environments people must inhabit and the institutional changes that could reduce harm without pretending one social explanation accounts for every life.

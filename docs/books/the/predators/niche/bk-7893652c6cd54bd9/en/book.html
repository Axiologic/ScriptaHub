The Predator’s Niche

Psychopathy, Artificial Minds, and the Possible Split of Humanity

Cooperation becomes vulnerable when understanding others is separated from concern for them. This book examines the environments that can reward such asymmetry. Psychopathy, institutions, and artificial minds frame its inquiry into predatory strategies. Trust, incentives, and the ability to refuse remain closely connected questions. Information and opportunities for repair are examined alongside power. Protecting cooperation requires examining conditions rather than dividing everyone into fixed moral types.

About Book

Trust makes cooperation possible. It also creates opportunities for anyone who can understand other people without sharing their concern. This book asks what kinds of environments allow that asymmetry to become power.

Moving through psychopathy, institutions, and artificial minds, it examines predation as a role or strategy that systems may enable. The analysis questions simple divisions between human types and turns instead toward incentives, information, repair, and the ability to refuse. For readers interested in social power and AI, it offers a way to think about cooperation that takes exploitation seriously without treating suspicion as the basis of every relationship.

About Book

This history begins by imagining a present without the internet, then moves to the people and institutions that helped build it. It follows Licklider’s network vision, Baran’s and Davies’s packet switching, Kleinrock’s research, and Cerf and Kahn’s TCP/IP. ARPANET becomes a working network, while NSFNet and regional connections broaden its reach.

The book distinguishes early military and academic aims from later uses in communication, research, commerce, education, entertainment, and public services. Its closing chapters consider privacy, unequal access, net neutrality, misinformation, and governance. The opening scenario is explicitly imaginary; the historical chapters trace a collaborative technological process.

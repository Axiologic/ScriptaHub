About Book

The Messiah Factory examines how a persuasive public identity can acquire more authority than its evidence warrants. It begins by separating attention, credibility, decision-making power, performance, and welfare. The chapters then ask what happens when correcting a public claim threatens relationships, work, or a group’s sense of purpose. Research on prestige, uncertainty, social influence, and collective judgment informs the argument, but the book does not present its overall thesis as an experimentally established law.

Its proposed alternatives include staged evaluation of contributions, a problem-centered discovery system whose AI component must remain contestable, and “incomplete tribes” that permit serious cooperation without claiming every part of a member’s identity. The final chapter sketches studies that could disconfirm these ideas. Readers interested in leadership, epistemic institutions, group belonging, and AI-assisted deliberation will find a sustained argument about how commitment can remain open to correction.

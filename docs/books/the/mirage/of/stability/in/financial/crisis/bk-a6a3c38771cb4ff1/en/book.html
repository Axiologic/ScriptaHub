About Book

This book explores the recurring pattern of confidence, speculation, panic, and intervention in financial crises. Historical chapters discuss Dutch tulips, the 1929 crash, the dot-com bubble, and the 2008 housing collapse. The author links these events to investor psychology and the danger of overlooking fundamental risks during apparent prosperity.

Later chapters examine lower interest rates, quantitative easing, fiscal stimulus, and the difficulty of judging their long-term effects. Decisions by central banks also cross borders through capital flows, exchange rates, and debts denominated in foreign currencies. The conclusion calls for vigilance, transparency, and cooperation while recognizing that measures taken to stabilize a crisis can create later vulnerabilities.

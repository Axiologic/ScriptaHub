The Blind Specialist

Why Our Systems Create Collective Blindness and How Modern Tribes Can See Again

The Blind Specialist examines the blind spots created by successful expertise. Surgeons, engineers and security researchers each see through powerful professional lenses. Problems between their fields can escape every individual specialist. Institutions can reinforce these narrow views even when their members are competent. The central concern is connecting knowledge without imagining an all-knowing generalist. Cooperation across professions becomes a question of responsibility for the wider world.

About Book

Expertise works because it leaves things out. The surgeon, engineer, and security researcher each inherit a powerful lens that makes particular problems visible. Trouble begins when no one can see what falls between those lenses.

This book examines how successful specialization can produce collective blindness, especially when a way of seeing becomes an institution. It asks how modern communities might connect expertise without pretending that one generalist can know everything. For readers working across professions, organizations, or complex social problems, it offers a challenge: keep the microscope, but build the relationships that help its user remember the world around it.

About Book

This book examines how investment managers can gain influence over companies and markets while acting on behalf of many separate clients. It follows ownership stakes, proxy voting, board engagement, fund flows, and the growth of index investing. Its historical account connects scale to lower fees, technology, and consolidation, while asking who actually makes consequential decisions.

Later chapters consider common ownership, conflicts of interest, responsible-investment claims, and possible checks on concentrated power. Disclosure, regulation, alternative ownership, and voting accountability all involve tradeoffs. The book presents a contested industry rather than a single villain, and ends by asking whether investors, firms, regulators, and citizens can shape a more accountable financial system.

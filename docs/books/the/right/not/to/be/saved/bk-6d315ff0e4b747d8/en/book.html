About Book

Can knowledge arrive too early, or help become an imposition? This book starts with closed doors, initiation, and the uncomfortable possibility that immediate disclosure does not always increase a person's freedom.

Historical examples of restricted knowledge open a wider inquiry into power, preparation, and the dangers of forced good. The argument asks when protective limits serve those affected and when they merely preserve authority. For readers interested in ethics and the politics of expertise, it brings a difficult right into view: the ability to contest the intervention of people who are certain they know what would save you.

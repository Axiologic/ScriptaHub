The Substrate Cycle

Two Stories of Abundance, Power, and the Ownership of Reality

Two linked novels explore societies where money no longer governs basic survival. Reputation, infrastructure, and biology become arenas for disputes over shared reality. The cycle examines who shapes the conditions of future choices. Abundance and freedom meet questions about influence, dependence, and consent across generations. Solving practical problems brings questions about the authority earned through that work. Political choice extends to the biological and infrastructural foundations of lives still to come.

About Book

Money is no longer the main obstacle to food, shelter, or production. Power has moved into the systems that decide which problems count, which futures are possible, and whose judgment shapes the shared world.

These two linked novels explore abundance through reputation, infrastructure, biology, and the ownership of reality itself. Those who solve dependencies gain influence, but may also gain reasons to preserve or create new dependencies. For readers drawn to political science fiction, the cycle asks what freedom requires when society can redesign the conditions on which future choices depend, long before the people affected are able to consent.

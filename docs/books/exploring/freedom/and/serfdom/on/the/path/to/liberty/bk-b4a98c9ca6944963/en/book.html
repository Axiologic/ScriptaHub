About Book

This book interprets Friedrich Hayek’s The Road to Serfdom through the relationship between economic choice and personal liberty. It introduces free markets and individual responsibility, then explains the knowledge problem faced by central planners: no small authority possesses all the local information needed to direct an economy. The account uses Soviet shortages and twentieth-century totalitarian regimes as warnings about concentrated power.

It argues that restrictions introduced for security can accumulate and threaten other liberties. Its final chapters propose education, competition, legal protection for property and contracts, and civic participation as ways to defend autonomy. The presentation reflects Hayek’s argument while inviting readers to examine its historical comparisons.

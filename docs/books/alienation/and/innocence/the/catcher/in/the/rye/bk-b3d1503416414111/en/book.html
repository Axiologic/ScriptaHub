A study of Holden Caulfield’s loneliness, desire for connection, and fear of growing up

About Book

This essay interprets Holden Caulfield’s alienation in Salinger’s novel as a conflict between longing for connection and distrust of the adult world. Beginning with his expulsion from Pencey Prep, it follows his critical judgments of school, family, and social expectations. New York offers repeated encounters with Sally Hayes, Sunny, the nuns, and Carl Luce, but each leaves Holden more aware of his difficulty with vulnerability.

Phoebe and the unchanging museum focus his wish to save childhood from loss. The carousel scene offers the essay’s answer: he cannot prevent growth, but he can care for others while accepting their freedom.

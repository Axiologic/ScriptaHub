Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass

An American slave writes his own account of bondage and freedom

Douglass recounts childhood separation, plantation violence, and the daily control imposed by slavery. In Baltimore, learning to read reveals a path toward freedom. His resistance to Edward Covey changes his sense of himself. A failed escape plan precedes his successful flight north. The original preface, letter, and appendix frame his abolitionist testimony. A modern perspective introduces the historical context and major narrative threads.

About Book

Frederick Douglass’s 1845 autobiography follows his life from childhood enslavement in Maryland to literacy, resistance, escape, paid work, and public abolitionist speaking. Its first-person testimony describes the violence of slavery and the social arrangements that sustained it.

This edition preserves Douglass’s original chapters and appendix alongside the historical preface and Wendell Phillips’s letter. A separate modern perspective offers biographical context and reading guidance. The original text distinguishes the Christianity Douglass respected from the slaveholding religion he condemned.

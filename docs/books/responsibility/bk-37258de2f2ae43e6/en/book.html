Responsibility

What We Owe a World We Did Not Choose

This essay examines responsibility from childhood and ordinary decisions to institutions and divine creation. It distinguishes causing harm, answering for actions, and responding to suffering. Examples consider poverty, work, citizenship, scientific knowledge, and automated decisions. Philosophical arguments meet research on agency, cooperation, and collective behavior. The discussion explores obligations toward people who cannot answer for themselves. Its central question concerns what a finite person owes an unchosen world.

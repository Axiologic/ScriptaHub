About Book

After success, we call the founder a visionary. After failure, the same conviction can look like delusion. How should an ambitious belief be judged before the outcome makes uncertainty disappear?

This book examines inventors, entrepreneurs, and the organizations that give conviction money, followers, and authority. It asks whether a wager produces informative tests, protects dissent, and allows someone to stop escalation when evidence turns against it. For readers interested in innovation and leadership, it offers a way to defend the freedom to try while keeping confidence answerable to the people who may bear the cost of being wrong.

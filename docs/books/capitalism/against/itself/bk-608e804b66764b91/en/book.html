Capitalism Against Itself

Can a System Reform the Forces That Make It Powerful

This essay asks whether capitalism can reform the forces that sustain its power. It separates personal virtue from institutions capable of changing incentives. Chapters examine capture, incumbent delay, and conflicts across jurisdictions. The future appears as a party with weak bargaining power. The argument tests why successful reforms may later lose their force. It closes with questions about organized opposition and the limits of correction.

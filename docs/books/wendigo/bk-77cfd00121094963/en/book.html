About Book

In a winter camp, taking more than your share can become someone else's missing meal. The Windigo figure gives a disturbing face to appetite that crosses the obligations holding a community together.

This book begins with that cultural and ecological setting before exploring a wider question about systems that cannot recognize enough. Hunger, taboo, growth, and collective survival become lenses for examining civilization's appetite. For readers interested in myth and social analysis, it asks how a society might become unable to treat sufficiency as an achievement, and what is lost when the demand for more no longer has a meaningful stopping point.

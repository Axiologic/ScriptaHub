We Manufacture Our Own Masters

The Antinomies of Governance and the Refusal of Domination

This book asks how institutions built for freedom become instruments of control. It traces power through equality, markets, work, expertise, and public speech. Resentment and cultural reproduction complicate simple accounts of political progress. The chapters distinguish plausible mechanisms from historical evidence and speculation. Artificial intelligence raises new questions about ownership, dependence, and life beyond work. The argument tests whether organized counterpower can preserve the capacity to refuse.

About Book

The book begins with a paradox: institutions that promise emancipation can concentrate the power to define its terms. It examines socialist administration, capitalist closure, workplace hierarchy, censorship by noise, expert authority, and resentment without treating these systems as identical.

Later chapters ask whether liberal norms reproduce themselves, how force limits local autonomy, and what AI could change about dependence and status. The author separates personal experience, documented research, theoretical mechanisms, and speculation. The final question concerns counterpower: who can refuse an arrangement, at what cost, and for how long?

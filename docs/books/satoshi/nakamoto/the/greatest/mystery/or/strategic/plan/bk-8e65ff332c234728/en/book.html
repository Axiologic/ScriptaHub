About Book

The book places Bitcoin’s emergence beside the international role of the US dollar and the petrodollar. It traces the author’s view of oil trade, monetary dependence and the incentives a dominant state might have if its currency lost influence. From there, it proposes a provocative hypothesis: that American strategic interests could have shaped a seemingly independent digital currency. This is the book’s interpretation, not a verified account of Bitcoin’s origins.

The chapters consider the Satoshi Nakamoto pseudonym, mining and exchange infrastructure, competing states, and a possible transition toward digital payments. Some passages describe imagined events beyond the source’s 2024 date; they belong to the argument’s scenario rather than a record of events. The conclusion returns to the tension between decentralised freedom and concentrated power. Readers interested in monetary geopolitics can examine the chain of reasoning and judge where evidence ends and conjecture begins.

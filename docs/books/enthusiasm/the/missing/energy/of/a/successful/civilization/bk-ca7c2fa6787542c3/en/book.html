Enthusiasm: The Missing Energy of a Successful Civilization

Why a world rich in possibility can struggle to commit

The essay asks why abundant possibility does not always become meaningful action. It examines the success trap, early evaluation of dreams, and attention without direction. Enthusiasm emerges as voluntary energy directed toward a future one can help shape. Chapters consider community, work, scientific inquiry, culture, and artificial intelligence. The author also examines hype, burnout, identity traps, and compulsory passion. The closing chapters propose protected experiments and institutions that make participation consequential.

About Book

The essay begins by distinguishing stimulation from the sustained commitment that can move a worthwhile project forward. It asks why security, abundance, and capable institutions do not automatically give people a future they want to help build. Early chapters examine the success trap, dreams assessed before they have developed, and digital attention that repeatedly invites beginnings but rarely supports continuation.

The argument then defines enthusiasm as voluntary energy directed toward a meaningful, actionable possibility. It follows its social conditions through communities, work, science, culture, and AI, while examining hype, burnout, identity fusion, and coercive demands for passion. The final chapters consider small experiments and institutional permission: concrete ways to let people begin and remain responsible without discarding safeguards.

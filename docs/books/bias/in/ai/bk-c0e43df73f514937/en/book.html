About Book

An AI system learns from records, but records already contain decisions about who was seen, measured, hired, treated, or ignored. This book starts before the model, where institutions turn unequal experience into apparently ordinary data.

From classical machine learning to language models, it examines how bias enters through measurement, labels, proxies, and deployment choices. Familiar applications in health, hiring, and public life make the problem concrete. For practitioners and readers concerned with fairness, the book offers a way to ask better questions about a system's inputs and consequences, and to see why removing one sensitive attribute cannot settle the issue.

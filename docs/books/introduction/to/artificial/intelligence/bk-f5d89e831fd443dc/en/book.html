About Book

Albert Meta One introduces artificial intelligence through eleven linked chapters. The book begins with the field’s foundations and the rules, symbols, expert systems, and ontologies of early AI. It then explains how machine learning finds patterns in data, distinguishing supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning while discussing generalization, bias, and interpretability.

The middle chapters turn to neural networks, backpropagation, language models, and computer vision. Mathematical examples accompany the account of how models are trained. Later chapters compare forms of reasoning, planning, problem solving, and knowledge representation. The closing discussion considers privacy, accountability, employment, power, and the governance of future systems. Written from a 2024 perspective, this is an introductory survey of both technical approaches and their social consequences.

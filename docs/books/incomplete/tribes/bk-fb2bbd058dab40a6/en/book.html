Incomplete Tribes

Belonging and Conflict Among Imperfect Humans

Human beings need belonging, yet loyalty can turn neighbors into enemies. This essay proposes incomplete tribes: demanding communities that refuse total authority over their members. It traces how social identity, conflicting ideals, and institutional rules shape cooperation. Overlapping affiliations and protected dissent limit the power of any single group. Polycentric governance lets imperfect communities solve shared problems without claiming one final order. The closing chapters test this hope against artificial intelligence, technological risk, and the fear of failure.

About Book

This four-chapter analysis examines how Orwell’s Party controls Oceania. Telescreens, informants, and altered records keep citizens under pressure. Newspeak and doublethink narrow what people can express and verify.

Winston and Julia’s relationship tests the space left for trust. O’Brien’s interrogation shows power attacking memory and identity. The closing chapter connects language, truth, and individual freedom. The analysis treats the novel as a warning about institutions that can reshape a person’s sense of reality.

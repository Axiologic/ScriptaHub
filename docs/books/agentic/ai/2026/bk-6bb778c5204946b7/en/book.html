Essential Patterns and Beyond

AI agents turn language into actions that change files, tools, and workflows. Their design brings planning, memory, verification, and recovery into one system. Delegated work raises questions about visibility and control. The central concern is how to assess autonomy and recognize a completed task. Plausible language alone cannot establish that an action succeeded. The emphasis falls on execution that people can observe, limit, and check.

About Book

An AI system becomes an agent when its words can change what happens next. That simple boundary transforms software design: a plausible answer is no longer enough when a model can edit files, invoke tools, or keep acting on its own.

Explore the patterns behind agentic systems, from routing and planning to verification, memory, and recovery. The book examines why open loops fail and how a runtime can make delegated work observable and controllable. For developers and technical decision makers, it offers a practical vocabulary for choosing architectures, setting limits, and judging whether an agent has actually completed its task.

Two Ways to Build a World

Partnership, domination, and the technology of human cooperation

This essay revisits the partnership and domination contrast without assuming a peaceful prehistoric golden age. It treats both patterns as ways of coordinating people and allocating power. Chapters examine status, sanctions, exit, authority, and institutional scale. Archaeological claims are separated from the framework’s testable questions. The final chapters ask how AI can strengthen surveillance or lower the cost of cooperation. A scorecard and research agenda invite readers to test the argument.

About Book

The book reconstructs Riane Eisler’s partnership–domination contrast while challenging claims about a uniformly peaceful prehistoric Europe. It asks what remains when archaeology, anthropology, social psychology, and institutional economics are allowed to complicate the original story. Partnership and domination become patterns of coordination whose prevalence depends on resources, status, exit, sanctions, information, and the ability to contest power.

Eighteen chapters move from prehistoric evidence to prestige, coalitions, chokepoints, crises, governance, and artificial intelligence. The argument distinguishes necessary hierarchy from durable domination and treats technology as a multiplier of both cooperation and control. Two appendices offer an institutional scorecard and a research agenda. The manuscript presents disputed historical claims as hypotheses and asks readers to evaluate the framework against evidence rather than accept a moral label.

The Right to Copy: protecting creation while keeping knowledge usable

A guide to separating protected expression, useful meaning, trustworthy identity and the economic disruption of lawful substitutes.

What was actually taken?

The Right to Copy begins with two fictional competitors. Mara recognizes the distinctive teaching journey of her book beneath another book's changed sentences. Adrian sees a rival application perform the same useful tasks as his own, supposedly with newly generated code. Both have lost an advantage, but that fact alone does not identify a legal wrong. The book asks a more precise question: was the disputed taking a protected expression, a method, a reputation, confidential information or simply the benefit of arriving first? These losses demand different responses.

Copyright concerns qualifying expression rather than everything a reader learns. A protected book can contain unprotected facts, methods and conventional teaching sequences alongside original text, invented examples and creative arrangement. Software similarly mixes functional constraints with discretionary implementation choices. Effort and commercial value do not automatically make every component exclusive. Patents, secrets, trademarks and contracts protect different interests and carry different conditions. Identifying the asset and the act is more useful than claiming ownership of an entire product category.

The book separates five relationships: lexical overlap, expressive similarity, semantic equivalence, functional equivalence and commercial substitutability. These are analytical distinctions, not five statutory categories. Different words may preserve an elaborate narrative or unusual sequence of examples; a translation demonstrates how extensively wording can change while an adaptation remains. Conversely, an independently developed explanation can teach the same principle without appropriating its predecessor's expression. A rival manual may solve the same problem or reduce sales without copying protected material. Neither a similarity score nor a target percentage of changed words decides the question.

The relevant comparison therefore separates facts and methods, conventional or constrained elements, and potentially distinctive expression. It asks what was copied, whether that material is protected, whether the legally relevant threshold is met and whether a licence or exception applies. A renamed narrative may remain dependent, while an independently researched treatment may be legitimate competition. The analysis cannot stop at either the creator's sense of loss or the competitor's assurance that a machine produced new sentences.

One product, several acts and laws

The survey is educational, selective and tied to its research cutoff of 11 September 2026. Its international framework coordinates national systems rather than creating one world copyright code. Acts, locations, defendants, contracts and intended markets matter. A governing-law clause does not necessarily decide every noncontractual claim, and an international trademark application does not create an automatically enforceable worldwide mark. Practical enforcement also depends on evidence, counterparties and resources. A globally accessible service needs documented questions about real operations rather than a convenient server location presented as universal clearance.

AI processing is a supply chain of acts. Acquiring, storing, training, fine-tuning, retrieving, inserting text into a prompt, rewriting and publishing can involve different actors and permissions. Retrieval supplies selected external material while answering; it is not the same technical operation as changing model parameters. Lawfully obtained training material does not authorize every generated output. Independent wording does not cure unlawful acquisition. A data-flow map records what material goes where, for which purpose, under what retention and access conditions, and with which intended downstream uses.

National differences make this separation consequential. United States fair use is contextual rather than a fixed allowance or automatic educational permission. The EU's research-oriented and broader mining routes differ in eligibility, purpose, lawful access and reservations. AI-provider compliance duties and voluntary codes are not licences for every copyrighted work. Romanian contributor relationships and Swiss statutory categories require their own analysis. The book also distinguishes UK policy proposals from enacted amendments and Japanese interpretive guidance from universal permission. An agency report, treaty, judgment, settlement and technical standard must never be silently treated as interchangeable authority.

Permission itself must match the component and use. Buying a printed textbook does not automatically authorize scanning it into a private tutor. An open textbook can contain photographs excluded from its licence. Attribution does not authorize an otherwise restricted reproduction, and a recent translation can have rights separate from a public-domain original. The operational result should be a scoped conclusion attached to evidence, an activity, a jurisdiction and a review date, rather than an unrestricted approval label for the whole project.

A name that identifies responsibility

Trademarks address who stands behind goods or services. An independently branded functional competitor and a product falsely presented as Adrian's official release raise different issues. Names, domains, company registrations and trademarks are separate assets. Clearance must consider relevant markets and confusing similarities, not just identical strings; registration also needs maintenance and evidence of use. The mark identifies a source, but does not certify accuracy, security or responsible training. Its promise must be fulfilled by the institution using it.

Truthful comparison, criticism and compatibility claims require room to name another product. They differ from invented endorsement or a misleading official label. A disclaimer may not repair the overall impression of a deceptive page. For AI-generated interfaces and marketing, relationships such as made by, compatible with and approved by need documentary support. Blocking every competitor name would obstruct legitimate discussion; allowing every fluent affiliation claim would invite impersonation. Trademark should not become a substitute monopoly over content when a copyright claim fails.

Editorial responsibility also exceeds rights ownership. Copyright review asks whether use is authorized; attribution review asks whether intellectual debts are represented honestly; factual review asks whether statements are true. A licensed text may be false, and a noninfringing paraphrase may still hide scholarly dependence. Repeating an unusual error can help show dependence without turning surrounding facts into property. A trustworthy edition or software brand should identify actual review, maintenance and correction, not merely place a premium name on unexamined output.

Software that behaves the same

Software comparisons need to filter functionality and constraints before evaluating expressive correspondence. Two implementations may share a standard algorithm or required interface while independently expressing it. Distinctive comments, unnecessary structures or unusual errors present different evidence. Code, documentation, artwork, dependencies and data each need attention. Behavioral equivalence cannot substitute for legal independence: a buggy copy may behave differently, while an independent implementation may pass the same tests. Tests themselves establish results only for specified observations, inputs and environments.

Interoperability can protect users' ability to move their work between systems. The book distinguishes the EU functionality analysis in SAS from the particular fair-use holding in Google v. Oracle; neither becomes a universal statement that every interface or implementation is free. A lawful specification and documented independent implementation can support a defensible process. Clean-room separation helps preserve evidence but cannot legalize an unlawfully obtained specification or remove patents, contractual limits and protected material. Feeding proprietary code to an assistant and requesting independence does not establish it.

Open source supplies permissions with terms, not abandoned ownership. Notices, source obligations, patent provisions and network-use conditions depend on the actual licence and activity. Removing a header does not erase obligations attached to copied expression, while independently implementing a function does not inherit every similar program's licence. Matching tools should explain correspondences for review rather than announce infringement. A component inventory helps identify what is shipped, and signatures help preserve integrity, but neither proves ownership. Rights, behavior and security consequently need separate release gates.

Evidence a team can maintain

A component-level rights ledger connects each version to its creator or source, claimed owner, agreements, permitted activities, territories, restrictions and unresolved questions. Manuscript, cover, photograph, contractor code and dependency cannot be cleared by one unsupported statement that the organization owns the product. Authorship, ownership, assignment and licence also differ. AI-assisted works require an accurate account of perceptible human creative contribution; prompt quantity and an output-ownership clause cannot manufacture copyright where legal requirements are unmet or extinguish somebody else's rights.

Technical provenance has a narrower but valuable role. Hashes compare bytes; timestamps and signatures support chronology and authenticated assertions within their assumptions. A valid signature can still authenticate a false ownership claim. Rights-reservation signals communicate positions under relevant rules; crawler instructions are neither access authorization nor security. Matching and watermarks need evidence against stated transformations and false-positive risks. Preserving every private prompt indefinitely can create new confidentiality problems. A proportionate record connects technical artifacts with actual agreements and review decisions, retaining what a real dispute would need.

The proposed operating plan first inventories assets and prioritizes gaps, then establishes permitted AI inputs, component checks and edition reviews, and finally rehearses complaints and corrections. Approval is always for a stated use; conditional and unresolved entries make remaining work visible. Changes of territory, model configuration or version can trigger reassessment. Teams should measure their ability to reconstruct permissions, meet notice obligations and correct verified errors, rather than reward zero similarity. The aim is a defensible maintained process, not a certificate of invulnerability.

Compensation, competition and proportionate remedies

Mara and Adrian ultimately need two diagnoses: unlawful appropriation or deception, and sustainability under lawful substitution. Litigation may address the first while leaving the second untouched. Cheaper generation does not eliminate the value of checked sources, maintenance, support, migration or accountability, although customers will value these differently. Nor should maintenance become an excuse to trap users. Shared standards and open components can support competition while raising a genuine question about who finances costly upstream work and common infrastructure.

Licensing can authorize specified uses and compensate rights holders, but aggregation requires accurate repertoires, representation, withdrawal and distribution rules. Similarity to an output, causal contribution to training, legal entitlement and socially desirable reward are distinct allocation questions. A technical contribution score cannot enact a royalty regime or settle fairness. Public funding, institutional subscriptions, open licences and paid verified editions offer different possibilities, with risks of cost, concentration and capture. Their merits depend on whether they sustain quality, access and participation rather than simply produce more licences.

Enforcement should identify the claimant's authority, relevant version, protected correspondence and requested remedy, with meaningful opportunities to contest mistakes. Semantic resemblance alone is a poor removal rule. False positives burden educators and smaller defendants; false negatives harm creators. Correction of affiliation, compliance with notices, replacement or licensing of a component may address a problem more precisely than deleting an entire product, while serious cases can still justify formal remedies. Protecting the boundaries of rights means enforcing valid claims inside them and preserving criticism, learning and independent improvement outside them.

The book rejects a general monopoly over equivalent meaning while insisting that changed words or machine assistance cannot excuse appropriation. Law defines rights and limits, engineering preserves usable evidence, and institutions support quality, correction and fair participation. None performs the others’ work. Creators need remedies against copying and deception; readers and developers need freedom to understand, criticize and build something new. Keeping both possible is the central task when imitation becomes cheap.

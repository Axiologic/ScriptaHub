Anti-Smoothing: Clients, Markets, and Business Model

Ten-minute edition

1. The business thesis

Anti-Smoothing asks whether a summary, offer, or report has lost a condition that could change a decision. The proposed service returns a reviewable record of claims, evidence, limitations, and responses. The book tests three ways to operate it: a focused startup, an integrable product, and later a shared brand with independent operators. None is treated as validated demand.

2. What a customer buys

A material omission is more than optimistic wording: it can affect acceptance, price, timing, responsibility, testing, or execution. A bounded dossier identifies the difference, points to its sources, and allows correction or appeal. A bilateral Safe would give counterparties a shared procedure. It would not judge whether a person is honest or claim to discover every missing fact.

3. Choosing the first market

The proposed entry market needs a consequential decision, accessible documents, a buyer with a plausible budget, and errors that can be corrected. Repetition matters for a subscription. The author rejects covert employee investigations, demands for a favorable verdict, and promises to label people liars. A large industry alone does not establish a reachable initial market.

4. From AI pilot to contract

The principal proposed niche is AI and software suppliers moving from pilot acceptance to a contract or expanded deployment. A review would compare a demonstration and proposed terms with datasets, security requirements, integrations, human intervention, performance limits, and dependencies. It begins as a narrow, file-based service, with a person evaluating material findings before they become a decision report.

5. Research reports

A second possible niche compares research materials with the recommendation delivered to a client. Interview notes, observations, minority views, sample limits, and the distinction between observation and inference can all matter. The launch scope is bounded B2B market, user-experience, or commercial research; it does not promise comprehensive medical or scientific validation.

6. Later extensions

An API might return located findings and limits to software that already creates summaries. Technical transaction review and project portfolios are other possible extensions. These should follow evidence from paid delivery, not precede it. The service should not sell intent detection, universal certification, unlimited surveillance, or a guarantee of zero omissions.

7. Competition and positioning

Existing alternatives include internal review, consultants, general AI assistants, checklists, and accepting risk. The manuscript cites adjacent tools but supplies no comparative benchmark proving Anti-Smoothing superior. A defensible offer would have to demonstrate useful tests, specialist interpretation, integration into work, a traceable record, and an appeal procedure accepted even when the report is unfavorable.

8. Value worth paying for

The author separates saved review time, better commercial decisions, procedural recourse, and clearer understanding of uncertainty. Findings should be tracked as accepted, corrected, contested, or unresolved, with a record of what action changed. A decision to abandon a weak deal in time may also have value. Fees must not depend on closing the deal or reaching a favorable verdict.

9. Packages and prices

The proposed billing unit is a dossier or project flow, not each alert or monitored employee. Packages define source and claim limits, expert hours, clarification, and support; larger work must be authorized separately. The stated euro prices are hypotheses to test through paid pilots, not observed willingness to pay. A dedicated local installation has additional security, maintenance, and support costs.

10. Customer access

The opening message should concern a real decision about to be approved: what was lost between evidence and conclusion? A complete demonstration would show synthetic sources, a finding, a correction, and an accepted appeal. Direct contact with qualified firms and relevant delivery partners precedes broad promotion. Invitations, active use, and paid orders should be counted separately.

11. Sizing the market

There is no measured statistical market for Anti-Smoothing subscriptions in the book. The author builds hypothetical revenue envelopes from organization counts, eligibility filters, proposed prices, and sensitivity tests, then calls for replacement with qualified-account evidence. Country-level examples are reference points, not proof of a launch market or customers' willingness to pay.

12. Startup economics

Expert review, support, computing, infrastructure, and appeals must be costed from the bottom up. Contribution margin is not net profit; a twelve-month sales and cash scenario is a planning hypothesis, not existing traction. The proposed sequence is to sell bounded dossiers, measure delivery, automate repeated work, and only then expand distribution.

13. Startup or shared brand

A direct startup can first prove value in one niche without a coalition. A later operator network might share tools, standards, and a brand while keeping delivery independent and allowing clients to move. Its membership rules, fees, sanctions, and appeal process would require a viable governance model. Decentralization is not, by itself, evidence of neutrality.

14. Delivery and trust

The proposed workflow starts with authorized intake, extracts claims with source references, compares material conditions, and submits consequential observations to human review before controlled distribution. Citation and numerical checks should be deterministic where possible; plausible invented references are unacceptable. Document rights, confidentiality, prompt injection, contestation, and hidden preparation costs affect both trust and margin.

15. Launch and investment tests

During the first ninety days, the plan narrows to one niche and document family, two demonstrations, interviews with qualified buyers, a few paid reviews, and measured delivery. Investment and expansion depend on repeat purchases, contribution after human effort, distribution evidence, and corrected error rates. The conclusion supports a bounded commercial experiment, not a claim that broad demand or a decentralized infrastructure has already been proved.

16. The two practical annexes

Annex A models a decision dossier: an accepted pilot result is not automatically evidence of autonomous operation, expanded scope, or resolved dependencies, and a counterparty may challenge a finding. Annex B proposes an initial AI-expansion review at EUR 1,500 per file. It is an example offer to test, not a validated market price.

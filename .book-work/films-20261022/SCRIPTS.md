# Scripturi pentru filmele noi (în revizuire înainte de voce)

Fiecare: 3 scene (de ce → cum → ce), o propoziție pe subtitrare, sub 2 minute. Narațiune în engleză.

## A Mind Does Not Fit in a Feed

*A column of short feed fragments is crossed by one continuous orange thread that gathers them and leaves the column whole.* · 149 cuvinte · `presentations/a-mind-does-not-fit-in-a-feed/`

**WHY — Full day, unfinished idea**

1. You can meet many ideas in one day and still not say what you understood.
2. A Mind Does Not Fit in a Feed asks why abundance can leave an idea unfinished.
3. Its adversary is not the short form, but the impression of understanding.
4. It defends intellectual continuity: time for a question to develop and face contradiction.

**HOW — Evidence before the verdict**

1. Chapter One calls a fragment's hidden premises and alternatives “context debt,” a working concept.
2. Chapter Two separates research findings, plausible interpretations and normative recommendations about attention.
3. Chapter Three holds books to account: padding, false coherence and borrowed authority.

**WHAT — A thought you finish**

1. Chapter Four turns agreement into action: one small, verifiable change, proposed rather than proven.
2. Chapter Five lets blogs, short explanations and stable editions cooperate instead of compete.
3. Read it to learn when you have truly earned the words “I understood.”
4. Begin with the prologue, “A full day, an unfinished idea,” and stay with one question.

## Affective Calculus

*A radial appraisal vector whose shape shifts when one fact changes, moving the dominant point from fear toward anger.* · 147 cuvinte · `presentations/affective-calculus/`

**WHY — Not just a word**

1. Affective Calculus asks whether an AI can explain an emotion, not merely name it.
2. A character can say “I’m perfectly fine” in a scene of despair.
3. An AI judge hunting for emotion words mistakes that expression for the actual state.
4. The book treats emotion as an evaluated relationship between an agent and its world.

**HOW — The appraisal vector**

1. Its proposed Appraisal Affective Calculus first separates brief episodes from moods, sentiments and attitudes.
2. An appraisal vector then records goals, control, agency, norms, dignity and attachment.
3. Labels become probabilistic prototypes, with intensity and confidence reported separately.

**WHAT — Change one fact**

1. Mara faces a running dog, a locked gate and a dead-end alley: fear.
2. Now she can shut the gate, and the owner freed the dog deliberately: anger should rise.
3. Such minimal pairs reveal whether an AI explains emotion or only matches words.
4. Read it for that test, starting with Chapter 1, “An Emotion Is Not a Word.”

## Enthusiasm: The Missing Energy of a Successful Civilization

*A circuit runs from a small present-world terminal to a larger possible-future terminal, while scattered sparks of stimulation flicker without joining the current.* · 139 cuvinte · `presentations/enthusiasm-the-missing-energy/`

**WHY — The missing voltage**

1. Modern systems can run remarkably well without anyone feeling inspired.
2. This essay asks why a civilization so rich in possibility struggles to commit.
3. Its answer is enthusiasm: voluntary energy toward a future you can help shape.
4. Stimulation keeps nudging attention; voltage is the sense that effort moves something worthwhile.

**HOW — Traps and ignition**

1. The author shows how safeguards, metrics and early evaluation make beginnings costly.
2. Dreams get judged as credentials or business models before they gain depth.
3. Ignition needs a reachable first step, visible consequences and enough control to change the work.
4. The book also confronts cults, bubbles, burnout and the unpaid passion tax.

**WHAT — Permission to begin**

1. It proposes protected experiments, honest work and communities with room for dissent.
2. Even generative AI is weighed: who still chooses, checks and stays with the result?
3. If your ideas lack a credible first move, begin with “The Missing Voltage.”

## Incomplete Tribes

*An archipelago of distinct islands of different sizes, joined by several bridges so that no single island becomes the whole world.* · 150 cuvinte · `presentations/incomplete-tribes/`

**WHY — Good People First?**

1. Incomplete Tribes is an essay on a dangerous question: must humanity become good before civilization works?
2. Its answer is architectural: change the design of belonging, not human nature.
3. We love, build and mourn in groups, yet loyalty can turn neighbours into enemies.

**HOW — Deep, Never Total**

1. Chapter Nine defines an incomplete tribe: thick cooperation without total authority over members.
2. A chess club may govern tournament conduct, but not a member's romantic life.
3. Other loyalties count as safeguards, so no group becomes the sole source of truth and affection.
4. Exit and dissent stay open, so disagreement is not treated as betrayal.

**WHAT — Bridges, Not Walls**

1. Overlapping loyalties make people harder to sort into clean enemy camps.
2. Peace becomes an institution that lets rivals lose without being erased.
3. Chapter Fifteen tests it against AI that could become the sole interpreter of a member's world.
4. Start with the Prelude, The Question Beneath the Question, and find hope without an ideal human.

## Is There Light at the End of the Tunnel?

*Nested tunnel arches narrow toward a small warm opening, where light stands for institutions that can still be challenged and corrected.* · 155 cuvinte · `presentations/is-there-light-at-the-end-of-the-tunnel/`

**WHY — When the exit resembles the entrance**

1. This political essay begins with an unease: systems promising freedom rebuild the dependencies they opposed.
2. Equality can conceal a caste, and economic freedom can buy political power.
3. The initiator's experience of communism serves as testimony and a source of questions, not proof.

**HOW — Power changes its costume**

1. Its real question is architectural: who can compel whom, and who can replace them?
2. Privilege lives in office, careers, information and status, and each can convert into another.
3. When winners can rewrite the rules, market reward becomes political power.
4. Cited findings, possible mechanisms and working proposals stay apart, so you can weigh the evidence.

**WHAT — A constitution open to correction**

1. It proposes constitutional cosmolocalism: global knowledge, distributed capacities, and rights beyond local goodwill.
2. Artificial intelligence and basic income are tested by who owns, who controls, and who speaks.
3. Its light is modest: institutions where errors become information and leaders can be replaced peacefully.
4. Start with the introduction, When the Exit Resembles the Entrance, then test the ten clauses yourself.

## The Messiah Factory

*A crowd of small audience dots feeds a rising beam that inflates a halo above a single podium, while a deliberate gap left open in the halo marks room for correction.* · 147 cuvinte · `presentations/the-messiah-factory/`

**WHY — Who gets heard**

1. The Messiah Factory asks why the power to persuade keeps passing for understanding.
2. Its suspicion is that becoming audible may reward fixed answers over revisable thought.
3. Yet the book also refuses the flattering story of the ignored genius.
4. Its real subject is how coordinating a group becomes control over what it may question.

**HOW — Audiences make prophets**

1. It first separates attention, credibility, authority, performance and welfare into different contests.
2. Audiences reward some versions of a speaker until that persona becomes the speaker's livelihood.
3. The danger begins when no evidence can genuinely reduce the center's authority.

**WHAT — Cooperation without prophets**

1. Its proposed discovery system weighs claims rather than names, and must never become an oracle.
2. Its incomplete tribes offer real shared work while protecting disagreement, outside ties and departure.
3. Read it to learn how commitment can survive correction without waiting for a messiah.
4. Begin with “Who Gets Heard,” and ask which contest your trusted voices won.

## The New Social Contract in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

*A gold automated hub radiates services to independent nodes, each still connected by a link it can contest, cut or reroute.* · 163 cuvinte · `presentations/the-new-social-contract-in-the-age-of-ai/`

**WHY — Comfort without surrender**

1. Imagine an automated order offering comfort, safety and varied experiences, if you hand it governance.
2. This book takes that offer seriously, answering with twenty-one debatable clauses of a new social contract.
3. Its fear is not scarcity, but a leadership that controls automated infrastructure and cannot be replaced.

**HOW — Delegate, never surrender**

1. Administration may be delegated to automated systems, but collective goals must stay under legitimate control.
2. A right to leave a provider counts only if identity, records and services move too.
3. An appeal needs reasons, corrected facts, independent review and a real remedy.
4. Those who set goals, run infrastructure and oversee rights must remain separate powers.

**WHAT — Clauses under stress**

1. Later clauses protect attention, privacy, ecological budgets, reproductive freedom and room for new generations.
2. The final chapters ask who pays, who enforces, and why powerful actors would comply.
3. Its test is clear: abundance is legitimate when it reduces dependency, not when it makes dependency comfortable.
4. Begin with the Introduction, where the book sets out the political offer of an automated order.

## The Unfinished World

*A dark half-mapped globe at dawn, some surface tiles filled and others still dashed and open, shows the epilogue's image of reality having seams where effort can enter.* · 151 cuvinte · `presentations/the-unfinished-world/`

**WHY — A future grown small**

1. The Unfinished World opens with a strange diagnosis: our future has begun to look small.
2. We inherited machines that edit genes and reach other worlds, yet our mood is often fatigue.
3. This book argues for hope without denying climate risk, war, or concentrated power.
4. Its starting fact is simple: almost everything we care about is still unfinished.

**HOW — Hope without prophecy**

1. Here hope is not prediction; it is commitment to a possible good under uncertainty.
2. It asks whether an outcome is possible, valuable and influenceable enough to justify action.
3. Awe widens the horizon, agency finds a lever, and fellowship makes effort endurable.

**WHAT — Reality has seams**

1. Its frontiers run from an unmapped mind to a universe that is still mostly night.
2. Courage becomes rational when institutions make intelligent failure survivable.
3. Read it for a hope that survives tragedy because it never promised an ending.
4. Begin with the prologue, We Are Too Early to Give Up, and choose a frontier.

## Two Ways to Build a World

*A stepped rust pyramid stands beside a teal lattice of linked nodes, two buildable architectures of coordination taken from the book's cover.* · 152 cuvinte · `presentations/two-ways-to-build-a-world/`

**WHY — After the golden age**

1. A nearly forty-year-old idea claims societies keep organizing around partnership or domination.
2. Two Ways to Build a World keeps Riane Eisler's contrast and drops the peaceful golden age.
3. It treats domination and partnership as two recurring ways people coordinate under power.
4. Its central claim is that technology and institutions change which way is cheaper.

**HOW — From boxes to coordinates**

1. Instead of labelling whole civilizations, the book measures relationships along distinct dimensions.
2. It asks who can leave, speak, appeal, observe decisions, and impose sanctions.
3. Domination appears when wealth, media, office, and force convert into each other until escape disappears.
4. Hierarchy can stay legitimate when its purpose, scope, duration, review, and exit are clear.

**WHAT — Partnership needs teeth**

1. Drawing on commons governance, it shows cooperation needs boundaries, monitoring, graduated sanctions, and appeal.
2. Artificial intelligence can become an empire machine, or it can lower the cost of partnership.
3. Start with Chapter 3, From Binary to Coordinate System, and learn to map who holds power.

## We Manufacture Our Own Masters

*A stepped pedestal built from ordinary blocks raises a crown, while one small figure stands apart beside an open gap: the masters we build and the capacity to refuse them.* · 155 cuvinte · `presentations/we-manufacture-our-own-masters/`

**WHY — Ideals that breed masters**

1. Confiscate every fortune, and someone still decides who gets a home and who may leave.
2. We Manufacture Our Own Masters calls these traps antinomies, and finds them in every camp.
3. The author knew them from communism, where levelled incomes still hid a nobility without titles.

**HOW — The hostile-ruler test**

1. Its test is adversarial: judge each system by what a hostile leader could do.
2. Then ask how the people affected could stop that leader.
3. Liberalism, Chinese order, private cities and guaranteed income all face the same scrutiny.
4. Appendices separate supported claims from hypothesis, and the book turns its test on itself.

**WHAT — Building the power to refuse**

1. Markets can let winners close entry, and floods of noise can silence speech without censors.
2. Artificial intelligence may cheapen expertise while handing power to whoever owns the infrastructure.
3. A free order, the book argues, is measured by what citizens can refuse without starving.
4. Begin with chapter one, Antinomies Are Not Accidents, and run its test on your favourite system.

Blockchain and AI: Strategies for
Winning Enterprise Customers
Introduction
The report, "Blockchain and AI: Strategies for Winning Enterprise
Customers," offers a succinct overview of the author's personal experiences
and insights gained from establishing a startup that caters to large enterprise
clients. In an increasingly competitive landscape where supply often exceeds
demand, particularly for high-profile customers, it is essential to understand
the nuances of this challenging market.
Despite its briefness, the report encapsulates critical strategies that startups
can employ to attract and retain influential customers. Navigating this
complex environment requires a clear understanding of customer needs, the
ability to leverage innovative technologies, and the establishment of strong
relationships with industry leaders.
Unfortunately, attempting to serve customers who lack financial resources
can prove to be a losing proposition for entrepreneurs. This reality often
compels startups to enter a fiercely competitive "red ocean," where numerous
players vie for the same limited opportunities. To succeed in this
environment, it is crucial to differentiate oneself and offer unique value
propositions that resonate with potential clients. Through this contribution,
the author hopes to inspire other entrepreneurs and provide valuable insights
that can facilitate the successful development of startups in these dynamic
sectors.

Chapter One — Sales-Driven Startup Without a
Product
Building direct relationships with potential clients is important in the early
stages of a startup, even without a product. Through networking, meetings,
and presentations, startups can identify the specific needs of enterprises and
propose personalized solutions. This approach enables the creation of pilots
and MVPs (Minimum Viable Products) funded by clients, which can then be
tested and refined in collaboration with them, building trust and
strengthening market reputation. In fact, the most successful software
products are often those paid for by clients, rather than pre-built solutions
that struggle to find buyers.
Starting a startup in the fields of blockchain and AI doesn't mean beginning
from scratch. There are numerous open-source resources available that can
serve as a foundation for developing a prototype or demo. Utilizing
wireframing and design tools can effectively showcase your ideas and their
potential. Additionally, having a skilled team enhances this process, ensuring
that concepts are well-articulated and effectively presented.
A successful approach often involves understanding client needs first, then
creating solutions that address real problems rather than building solutions in
search of a problem. By organizing activities that focus on identifying these
needs and customizing existing solutions, startups can ensure that their
offerings are not only relevant but also directly tied to market demands.
Many successful companies, particularly in the tech space, have adopted this
model—focusing on the problem first, then building solutions in close
collaboration with their clients. This reduces wasted resources and increases
the likelihood of market fit from the outset.
A lean approach to entrepreneurship is supported by several key works. "The
Lean Startup" [1] by Eric Ries emphasizes the importance of creating a
Minimum Viable Product (MVP) and iterating rapidly based on customer
feedback, reducing wasted time and resources. "The Innovator’s Dilemma"
[2] by Clayton Christensen highlights the significance of disruptive
innovation, showing how startups succeed by addressing unmet needs in

emerging markets rather than pre-building solutions. His "Jobs to Be Done
Theory" [3] pushes entrepreneurs to deeply understand the real reasons why
customers use a product, focusing on solving actual problems. "Running
Lean" [4] by Ash Maurya builds on this by encouraging startups to validate
the problem before even developing a solution, ensuring product-market fit.
Similarly, "The Four Steps to the Epiphany" [5] by Steve Blank introduces
the "Customer Development" methodology, which advocates for validating
ideas with real customers to shape the product around market demands.
"Disciplined Entrepreneurship" [6] by Bill Aulet provides a structured, 24step process for building a successful startup, emphasizing a disciplined,
iterative approach. Finally, "Crossing the Chasm" [7] by Geoffrey Moore
discusses the challenges of moving from early adopters to the mass market,
reinforcing the need to adapt strategies at different stages of a product’s
lifecycle. Together, these works advocate for a customer-driven, iterative
approach where the focus is on solving real problems rather than building a
product upfront.

Chapter Two — Non-Profit Umbrella for an
Enterprise Startup
A promising model for promoting a startup is through establishing a nonprofit professional community. This approach brings together industry
professionals to collaborate on ideas, research, and product development. By
charging a membership fee, the startup generates income to sustain itself in
the early stages while focusing on refining its offerings. The platform
facilitates knowledge exchange and networking, serving as a launchpad for
commercial projects that bolster the startup’s market position.
A key element is an educational component, especially as emerging
technologies like AI and Blockchain require decision-makers to be well
informed. The startup can organize workshops, courses, and discussions,
providing valuable learning opportunities for potential clients while gaining

insights into their challenges. This creates a mutually beneficial setup where
clients effectively fund their own education and engagement with the
technology.
It's crucial to start by bringing in 2-3 high-profile members, as their
involvement will lend credibility, making it easier for other companies in the
pharmaceutical industry—and potentially from other sectors—to join,
especially given that the membership fee is relatively modest.
For example, with 100 members each paying €2,000 or €5,000 annually, the
startup can establish a stable NGO that grows organically through strategic
efforts like building an online community, attending conferences, and
participating in industry events. This self-sustaining model fosters long-term
engagement and growth without significant upfront financial risks.
Initially, the startup is not selling a product but rather offering benefits to
individuals within potential client organizations. The goal is to empower
these individuals to advocate within their companies, convincing their
employers to pay the membership fee—often several thousand euros—to
access the value offered by the community. The focus should be on what
individuals gain personally, such as opportunities for networking,
professional growth within their organization or the broader industry, and
ways to differentiate themselves from their peers.
By joining the community, members can access exclusive knowledge about
emerging technologies, gain early insights into industry trends, and
participate in high-level discussions that may position them as thought
leaders within their companies. Additionally, they can tap into a network of
professionals who face similar challenges, providing a space to share best
practices and collaborate on innovative solutions. These individual benefits
create a compelling value proposition, making it easier for members to justify
the membership fee to their organizations while also establishing the startup
as a key player in fostering both personal and organizational growth.

Chapter Three — Promotion and Funding Strategies
Participating in business accelerators and incubators could be a good idea.
These programs provide tech startups with mentorship, funding, and
networking opportunities. In the initial years, they offer vital resources and
exposure to potential investors and industry clients.
Offering training sessions and workshops on how AI and Blockchain can
improve businesses is another way to build relationships with potential
clients. These educational efforts can generate early revenue and establish a
network of interested parties.
Building an online community of specialists, particularly in industries like
pharmaceuticals, where professionals can discuss AI and Blockchain
applications, positions the startup as a thought leader. Investing in content
creation and moderation during the early years can increase engagement and
generate qualified leads.
In Europe, accessing European Research Grants could be a good strategy for
startups, particularly through programs that can cover up to 70% of product
development costs or even 100% for more theoretical or fundamental
research projects. These initiatives often take the form of public-private
partnerships with the European Union, where large companies, such as those
in the pharmaceutical sector, play a crucial role. In such collaborations,
private partners (for example potential customers) contribute 50% of the
project's value, which can be in the form of direct funding or in-kind by
allocating employees who work on the research project alongside their
regular duties. The European Union then matches this private contribution,
effectively doubling the available resources. A startup or a small consortium
can secure funding from the EU, while a consortium of large private entities
reduces their research costs and gains access to startups and academic
institutions that may possess missing AI skills. This collaboration can lead to
better solutions than those developed solely by internal teams. This approach
not only provides substantial financial support but also fosters collaboration
with key industry players, further enhancing the startup’s potential for growth
and innovation.

Chapter Four — Short Summary of Best Practices
Creating a successful startup in the AI and blockchain sectors necessitates a
strategic approach that encompasses various best practices. Understanding
customer needs is paramount; engaging directly with potential clients allows
startups to gather valuable insights into their specific challenges. By focusing
on real problems rather than assumptions, entrepreneurs can develop tailored
solutions that resonate with the market. This involves creating a Minimum
Viable Product (MVP) based on validated learning, ensuring that the solution
meets the actual demands of clients.
Embracing a lean approach is equally critical. By adopting a methodology
that prioritizes rapid iteration and validation of ideas, startups can minimize
wasted resources. This iterative process involves testing concepts in realworld scenarios and refining them based on user feedback. The goal is to
ensure that the final product effectively addresses the identified needs,
thereby increasing the likelihood of market acceptance.
Leveraging strategic partnerships can significantly enhance a startup's
credibility and reach. Collaborating with established industry players
provides access to resources, expertise, and valuable networks. Forming
consortia with larger enterprises enables startups to pool resources and
enhance their market presence. Moreover, engaging in mentorship
opportunities with experienced professionals can guide product development
and market entry strategies.
Utilizing the "Jobs to Be Done" framework is another effective strategy. By
understanding the underlying motivations behind customer choices, startups
can align their product offerings with client goals. This insight informs both
product development and marketing strategies, allowing for a more targeted
approach that resonates with potential buyers.
Fostering an educational community offers a dual advantage. Establishing a
non-profit professional network enables startups to promote themselves while
simultaneously educating potential clients about emerging technologies. By

organizing workshops and training sessions, startups can position themselves
as thought leaders, building trust within the industry and enhancing their
reputation.
Participation in accelerators and incubators provides startups with crucial
resources during their formative years. These programs often offer funding,
mentorship, and networking opportunities that can significantly boost
visibility and credibility. By taking advantage of these platforms, startups can
connect with potential investors and industry experts, paving the way for
growth.
Focusing on thought leadership is essential for establishing credibility in the
competitive AI and blockchain landscape. By creating valuable content that
addresses key industry issues, startups can engage with professionals and
foster a community of specialists. This engagement positions the startup as a
leader in the field, attracting interest and generating qualified leads.
Exploring funding opportunities, particularly through European research
grants, can bolster a startup's financial resources. These programs often cover
a substantial percentage of development costs, alleviating financial burdens
and enabling startups to invest in innovation.
Forming consortia with larger companies enhances access to these funds and
fosters collaboration that can lead to improved solutions.
Iterating based on market feedback is crucial for maintaining relevance in a
fast-paced environment. Startups must remain flexible and responsive to
changing market conditions, continuously adapting their products to meet
evolving customer demands. Monitoring industry trends ensures that the
solutions offered stay aligned with market needs, facilitating a smooth
transition from early adopters to the mainstream market.
Implementing a structured approach to entrepreneurship is vital for ensuring
comprehensive development. Following established frameworks, such as
those outlined in Disciplined Entrepreneurship, provides a systematic process

for addressing all critical aspects of startup growth. This methodical approach
ensures that entrepreneurs remain focused on key objectives, enabling them
to navigate the complexities of building a successful enterprise.
In addition to these foundational practices, several other strategies can further
enhance a startup’s potential for success. Prioritizing security and compliance
is essential, especially in the realms of AI and blockchain, where data
protection is paramount. By implementing robust security measures and
ensuring compliance with industry regulations, startups can build trust with
clients and mitigate legal risks.
Cultivating a culture of innovation encourages creativity and experimentation
within the organization. Empowering team members to explore new ideas
and view failures as learning opportunities fosters an environment conducive
to breakthrough innovations. Collaboration and open communication are key
to driving innovative solutions that can set a startup apart from competitors.
Leveraging data analytics is another critical practice. Utilizing data-driven
decision-making allows startups to gain valuable insights into customer
behavior and market dynamics. By analyzing this data, entrepreneurs can
refine their strategies and enhance their product offerings, ensuring alignment
with client needs.
Developing a scalable business model is crucial for sustained growth.
Startups should design their business plans with future expansion in mind,
considering how their solutions can adapt to different market segments.
Creating recurring revenue streams, such as subscription models, ensures
consistent income and supports long-term viability.
Finally, networking relentlessly is essential for establishing valuable
connections within the industry. Engaging in industry events, conferences,
and online platforms enables startups to forge relationships with potential
partners, clients, and investors. Maintaining these connections fosters
ongoing dialogue and opens doors to new opportunities.

By integrating these best practices into their strategies, startups in the AI and
blockchain sectors can effectively position themselves for success. Focusing
on customer needs, leveraging partnerships, fostering innovation, and
maintaining a commitment to security will help them navigate the
complexities of the marketplace. As these startups continue to evolve and
adapt, they can thrive and lead in the rapidly changing technological
landscape.

Conclusion
To succeed in the AI and Blockchain space, startups must employ a multifaceted strategy. Building direct relationships with potential clients, even
without a final product, allows for the development of tailored solutions and
the creation of MVPs that can be refined in collaboration with partners. This
early-stage approach builds trust and a strong market reputation.
Creating a non-profit professional community offers another avenue for
growth, attracting industry professionals and fostering collaboration on largescale projects. This model can generate early revenue through membership
fees, while simultaneously enhancing the startup’s visibility and influence.
Additionally, participating in business accelerators and incubators provides
essential mentorship, funding, and networking opportunities, offering critical
support in the formative years. Meanwhile, educational initiatives like
workshops and online communities help position the startup as a thought
leader in AI and Blockchain.
Finally, forming consortia to access European funding, such as programs
offered by the Innovative Health Initiative (IHI), is key to supplementing
private investments. This strategy not only provides significant capital but
also strengthens collaborations with major players in the pharmaceutical and
tech sectors. Together, these strategies offer a comprehensive roadmap for
building a robust, sustainable enterprise in the competitive world of AI and
Blockchain startups.

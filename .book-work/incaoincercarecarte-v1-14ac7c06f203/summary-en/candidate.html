One More Try

How to preserve curiosity while distinguishing persuasive possibilities from results that hold up beyond the conversation.

When almost finished means something else

Dana rolls again, Andrei asks an agent to repair almost-working code, and Mara develops an ambitious theory with a chatbot. These fictional scenes share the phrase “one more,” but not a single prize. Dana may want enjoyment, Andrei a dependable program, Mara a discovery. One More Try asks how we recognize a gain when language can continuously renew the promise of one. It does not equate AI with gambling or condemn persistence. It asks whether the next attempt preserves learning or merely replaces the scenery around the original hope.

Meaningful uncertainty differs from noise. Chance mechanisms, hidden information, another person’s intentions, limited calculation and unsettled interpretive criteria cannot all be resolved in the same way. Curiosity concerns something we care about, and exploration can be pleasurable without producing a marketable result. Repetition of familiar music can be pleasurable too. The book’s suggestion that structured openness invites exploration remains an interpretive hypothesis, not a universal psychological recipe. A surprising model response may reflect possibilities the user did not anticipate; an exactly repeated answer may still be wrong.

The proposed “semantic near miss” names a result with the recognizable shape of success but no reliable measure of distance to the property that matters. A program can look finished while missing authorization checks; a paper can have impeccable sections without valid evidence. Clear form helps criticism but cannot replace it. The analogy does not import a proven gambling mechanism: the near-miss research discussed distinguishes subjective reactions from behavioral persistence and does not support an inevitable compulsion to continue. Wonder, hope and a theory’s validity remain different things.

Control includes formulating a request, choosing among candidates and deciding what evidence acceptance requires. Multiple generations can be rational when they feed a real selection process. They become less informative when satisfaction is the only criterion for a factual task. Keep the original objective beside the actual result: finding a counterexample can be progress without proving the theorem. The book calls unchecked results treated as accepted “verification debt,” a conceptual label rather than a standardized measure. Regression tests and decision records preserve gains that a new version might otherwise erase.

Luck does not make every choice equally rational. A decision can improve the distribution of possible outcomes without determining the one that occurs. Judge it using the information available beforehand, including the possibility of catastrophic loss, rather than simply declaring a winner skillful. The same discipline applies to prompting: a vague request can accidentally produce an excellent answer and a careful request can fail. Compare practices across equivalent tasks before treating one memorable success as evidence that a particular ritual works.

The answer that agrees too readily

Being understood is not being right. An assistant can clarify an intuition while its editorial help appears to certify scientific importance. Sycophancy means excessive agreement, not ordinary kindness. The experimental findings discussed concern bounded effects on conviction and willingness to repair interpersonal conflict, not the onset of psychosis. A theory becomes insulated when every criticism is recast as proof of its radical value. Novelty, correctness and importance require different comparisons; three generated perspectives may repeat one dependency rather than supply three independent pieces of evidence.

A discovery deserves an adversary capable of changing the verdict. The book points to FunSearch as a bounded example of program generation selected through executable evaluation, demonstrating that an unreliable generator can contribute to verifiable work. A hypothesis dossier states the claim, scope, closest alternative, test and result that would force revision. Development examples must be separated from final evaluation, and changes after failure retain their history. Independent reviewers are fallible too; the aim is challengeable procedure, not another oracle. Encouragement can respect the researcher while refusing premature certification.

The clinical discussion draws a firm boundary between intellectual error and illness. Enthusiasm, absorption, difficult-to-control use and psychosis are not inevitable stages of one sequence. Unusual ideas alone do not establish a diagnosis, and the technical term “hallucination” does not attribute clinical experience to a model. A case report may reveal serious suffering and plausible amplification without establishing prevalence or a sole cause. Self-selected harmful-use accounts, provider estimates of conversational signals and simulated dialogues have different denominators and cannot be converted into a population-wide probability of illness.

Constant availability can help someone prepare for human support or help them avoid every disagreeing perspective. Evaluating a conversation’s trajectory therefore matters beyond judging a single kindly worded reply. The book proposes acknowledging distress without confirming unsupported explanations, with proportionate protections rather than surveillance or workplace diagnosis. Marked changes in sleep, functioning or reality assessment warrant professional attention; immediate danger calls for local emergency services. A debate, chatbot or digital break is not a substitute for needed care, and treatment should not be changed on their authority. These safeguards do not pathologize ordinary use.

Faster generation, unfinished work

Agents can accelerate production while increasing the volume of work undertaken. In Andrei’s fictional day, an export repair grows into extra projects, each needing integration, review and ownership. Waiting intervals fragment into supervisory decisions, and computer capacity for more agents does not create human capacity to examine their outputs. New work can be valuable, but an unplanned prototype also creates obligations. Scope limits, small changes, protected tests and a separate list of future ideas can keep every possibility from immediately becoming a commitment; their effect still needs measurement.

Productivity evidence is heterogeneous. A controlled task completed from scratch and changes to mature repositories impose different costs, so favorable and unfavorable experiments need not contradict each other. The book also includes later updates explaining why changing participant selection and parallel agent use complicate measurement, alongside preliminary synthesis and self-reported estimates. None supplies an eternal speed multiplier. Faster implementation, better quality, new tasks, longer hours and easier-feeling work are different gains. Evaluation needs total time to an accepted result, later errors and costs transferred to reviewers, rather than the first impressive demonstration.

The proposed “expectations ratchet” occurs when exceptional demonstrations become standards for unlike tasks. It can be imposed by management or by a person who turns every new capability into a duty. Review queues may grow even while contributors work faster. Human control then risks becoming ceremonial: responsibility without enough time, evidence, competence or authority to refuse. Reduced routine effort can also concentrate consequential decisions and fatigue. These are questions about process and distribution, not proof of inevitable cognitive decline. Teams must preserve practiced judgment, mentoring and a genuine right to say what remains unverified.

Build acceptance around evidence

Verification starts by classifying the claim. Clearer prose needs an audience and editorial criteria; a sum needs data and execution; document support needs the exact passage and interpretation; code needs properties; fairness also needs values. A global score can hide an answer that is accurate but incomplete or useful but unsafe. The proposed architecture separates generator, evaluators, acceptance rule and log. The generator can repair failures but cannot quietly rewrite the finish line. A language judge remains a calibrated screening tool whose errors may depend on order and presentation, not an independent court of truth.

An answer with a verification dossier decomposes claims, names document versions and distinguishes explicit support, inference and missing evidence. Calculations run separately. A recorded fall in duration supports an arithmetic reduction without proving that a tool caused it or that all deadlines can shrink. Completeness requires another check: a summary of only true advantages may omit the decisive cost. Categories such as limits, adverse effects and contradictions can guide an omission search, while the choice of importance remains contestable. A hash or preserved source establishes integrity or provenance, not truth, and auditing must protect confidential material.

For software, translate intention into properties that would reveal the relevant failure. A booking system needs capacity, duplicate-request and access rules, including concurrent events; isolated happy-path examples test a narrower problem. Property-based tests seek counterexamples, relationships between executions test invariants, and a simple reference implementation offers another comparison. Agreement is not proof when both implementations share a mistake. Protected acceptance tests and new final cases reduce score optimization. Limited permissions and isolation reduce consequences, while reviewed changes, reproducibility, monitoring and rollback extend responsibility beyond the first green result.

Formal checking can provide stronger evidence: an explicit derivation is checked against rules and assumptions rather than admired for its typography. But admitted claims, inappropriate axioms or a model that assumes a perfect sensor limit the promise. Proving an implemented policy does not prove its fairness. Explain the formalization back to the people who own the requirement, including what was not proved. Statistical testing likewise requires a fixed version, relevant distributions and independence; observing no errors does not establish zero risk. Severity, detectability and reversibility matter beyond an average rate. Mechanizing checks leaves the choice of purposes and responsibility unresolved.

Asking a model to think again is not necessarily introducing new evidence. Tool-assisted correction can instead execute a calculation or inspect a source, changing what the process knows. Nor does a generated confidence percentage explain its own reliability. Calibration compares expressed confidence with outcomes across comparable tasks, separates severe errors and tests changed conditions. Abstention must remain a possible verdict when evidence is insufficient. A system can be accurate on many minor details while missing the one fact that changes the recommendation; acceptance cannot safely disappear into an attractive average.

A workshop with an ending

Organizations should distinguish exploration, candidates, partial verification, acceptance and rejection, and reserve stronger thresholds for actions with greater consequences. Measure review burdens and autonomy alongside quality and delivery time. Helpful friction is proportionate; endless confirmations encourage automatic approval. Shared sources, versions and criteria allow people to identify whether they disagree about facts, inference or values. Personalized confirmation otherwise risks producing separate appearances of authority without common grounds for challenge. The book presents these arrangements as governance and research proposals, not a completed system or universal legal checklist.

A session can deliberately explore, execute, evaluate or rest. Moving between modes is legitimate, but turning every evaluation into new possibilities prevents a verdict. Stop when the criterion is met, when progress stalls, when external evidence is needed or when fatigue undermines judgment. Ask what the next attempt could add: another formulation cannot create a missing source or replace a conversation with the affected person. Leave a short record of the accepted result, evidence, remaining uncertainty and next action. These are selective tools for consequential work, not a demand to audit every playful evening.

The casino metaphor must face evidence too: proposed studies would compare longer continuation with genuinely greater verified value. Those studies were not performed for the book. Its destination is a workshop where surprise remains welcome, failed hypotheses can teach and accepted outcomes have inspectable reasons. Sometimes the next step is another attempt; sometimes a test, another person or closing the window. Inexhaustible possibility need not determine when the day is enough.

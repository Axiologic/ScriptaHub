Symbolic World Models: continuity of meaning

How world patterns, instances, and interpreters can cooperate to track situations, revise conclusions, and connect decisions with effects.

A situation that continues after the answer

When someone reschedules a journey’s departure, the change does not stop at a date in the calendar. A transport connection, a meeting, or the time at which someone else expects us may become unsuitable. An assistant must recognize the situation, track dependencies, and distinguish exploring an alternative from requesting changes to reservations. The book proposes symbolic world models, SWM, for this continuity between language, reasoning, and activity. What matters is preserving meaning as the conversation and reality change.

Two concepts organize the proposal. A world pattern describes the objects, relationships, conditions, and operations reusable across a family of situations. A world instance preserves the particular case, with its information, alternatives, and relevant history. Changing a train’s time updates the instance; discovering that a connection may require crossing the city may require enriching the pattern. Repeated use does not automatically turn a piece of information into a rule. The neural model proposes interpretations, while symbolic components and interpreters check which consequences they permit.

The same reality can participate in different worlds. Within a company, scheduling a visit, preparing the bicycle, and researching its battery use distinct relationships. Confirming the meeting does not prove that the particular bicycle is ready, and a favorable experimental result does not confirm the visit. Separation follows the meaning of operations and need not coincide with departments. The worlds can be connected through the identity of the particular bicycle, the promised configuration, and the conditions of the demonstration. The architecture’s generality comes from its ability to connect differences while preserving what each result can support.

How meaning becomes an inspectable structure

A useful formalization shows which object we are discussing and what role the message plays. The expression “the eight o’clock train” may remain ambiguous between days or stations, and a request may ask only to save a scenario. Symbolic checking helps detect incompatibilities without replacing the interpretation of intention. The link to the original passage must be preserved: if the user corrects the day, the system can locate the changed reference and the calculations that depended on it. The interpretation therefore remains a proposal that can be examined.

SOP Lang provides a notation for this organization. A thread has an identifier, a type, and properties; references connect threads into a circuit. A person’s identifier allows meetings and journeys to concern the same person, even if someone else shares the displayed name. A reference and text reproducing its characters have different uses. The notation can describe both instances and patterns, but declaring a rule about journeys does not assert the existence of a particular journey. The meaning of operations belongs to interpreters and the application.

Matching searches for threads that jointly satisfy the required relationships. Shared variables must receive consistent values: the meeting found must belong to the correct person and day. If a branch fails, its provisional bindings are abandoned before the next attempt. This mechanism must not be confused with execution order. The semantic graph expresses the situation’s relationships, the dependency graph shows the values needed by a calculation, and the execution plan organizes operations for a question. A circular relationship between objects is not automatically a computational cycle.

Composition requires explicit interfaces, preserved identities, compatible units, and rules for transferring results. Validation checks syntax, references, types, and applicability conditions. An old timetable, however, can be perfectly valid structurally. Provenance and time remain necessary for evaluating content. A missing duration leaves the check open; incompatible units make the operation inapplicable; sufficient data that violate the deadline produce a negative result. These outputs have different meanings, and the system must preserve them through to the explanation given to the user.

Preparing the pattern includes checking available operations and building indexes that identify relevant rules when a new fact appears. This enables local updating: changing an arrival activates dependent temporal evaluations without requiring every calculation about every journey to be repeated. If a requested operation has no interpreter, the system must declare it unsupported. A condition does not become satisfied by omitting its calculation, and a missing input is not silently assigned a convenient value.

Reasoning and the question that changes the decision

Finding a feasible itinerary answers a question about possibility. It does not prove that the itinerary is the only one allowed, nor establish that it should be preferred. Necessity concerns all allowed alternatives, while preference requires criteria such as cost or risk. Deduction calculates the consequences of premises, constraints delimit acceptable combinations, and planning searches for steps whose preconditions and effects lead toward the goal. Each mechanism must state what it has established and the domain within which it explored the problem.

Stopping a calculation does not always mean the same thing. Monotone rules in a finite domain can reach a fixed point under the conditions specified by the engine. Exhausting a finite search can establish that the formulated problem is inconsistent, but exhausting the budget leaves the result unknown. Planning must also preserve branches in which a service refuses or does not respond. The existence of a favorable development does not guarantee that it will occur. The choice of action remains tied to the intention and authority expressed by the user, alongside the calculation’s result.

Abduction organizes possible explanations for an observed effect. A delayed train, a mistaken transfer estimate, or identifying a different room may explain a missed meeting. A candidate must be checked against accepted facts and against the rules’ ability to produce the effect. An explanation’s simplicity does not make it actual history. A useful observation distinguishes between candidates. Moreover, a statistical association may support prediction without specifying the effect of an intervention on the mechanism.

The same discipline guides dialogue. A useful question concerns the unknown that changes the answer to the current request, taking into account the effort of obtaining the information. When choosing a room, whether the discussion exceeds a threshold may matter without requiring its exact duration. If all admitted alternatives permit the same decision, the conversation can advance subject to their coverage. Returning to a topic requires retrieving the options discussed and checking subsequent changes. An option correctly retrieved may no longer be feasible.

Uncertainty cannot be reduced to a universal score either. A list preserves alternatives, an interval bounds values, and a probability distribution adds weights to outcomes. A probability requires the event and the model’s domain to be defined precisely; combining estimates requires the relationship between them and the dependence of the data. Decisions also use consequences and preferences. The same risk may be acceptable for a flexible visit and unsuitable before a presentation. A change of goal may require another choice without changing the estimate.

A memory that can change its conclusions

The same current value can conceal different histories. An educational program appears in only one school either because the original report was wrong or because other schools subsequently withdrew. The first situation corrects knowledge of the past; the second records a change in the activity. The instance preserves the subject of the claim, its period, source, receipt time, and role. The old report can remain in the history without continuing to be adopted as a premise. The explanation of yesterday’s decision is therefore not rewritten using information learned today.

Justifications make memory usable for revision. A conclusion identifies the rule and premises supporting it, while reverse indexes allow the consequences of a correction to be found. If an event can be streamed through a fixed connection or a suitable mobile connection, losing the former withdraws that path of support without necessarily canceling the conclusion. If all justifications disappear, dependents must be reevaluated. A circle of mutually confirming claims does not by itself provide an independent basis; support must be reconstructed from premises still adopted.

Uncertainty has distinct forms. Missing information, incompatible accounts, an interval of values, and a probability do not require the same calculation. An invitee’s missing confirmation does not prove refusal if the record is incomplete. The model’s confidence that it recognized an arrival estimate does not make that arrival certain. Several pages copying the same announcement are not independent observations. Combining estimates requires knowing the event measured, the conditions, and source dependence, while calibration is checked against observed behavior.

The action examined and the effect observed

An action’s description can be interpreted before execution. Shifting the start and end of a presentation equally preserves duration and shared breaks, but may exceed the room’s availability. Algebra examines preserved relationships, while symbolic execution separates program paths according to conditions. A path that cancels the old reservation before the new one is accepted may produce a counterexample: after refusal, no reservation remains. The analysis stays tied to the service’s actual contract and to the limits of exploration.

Concrete, symbolic, and abstract interpreters can use the same description with different results. Compact intervals may include the relevant executions while losing the relationship between two dependent values. An alarm obtained in this way requires refining the analysis before declaring a defect. The simulator and executor must be checked against the same semantics; a shared operation name does not guarantee equivalence. In parallel, neural models can provide perceptions, predictions, and memory candidates. Associating a detection with the tracked object and checking roles remain distinct operations.

A building’s digital twin makes these boundaries concrete. Temperature requires an object, a unit, an observation time, and provenance. A delayed measurement enters the history without automatically becoming the latest state. Heating simulation starts from an identified state and assumptions, and the calculated temperature remains a prediction, separate from the measurement and the organizer’s target. A difference between prediction and observation may trigger an investigation; by itself, it does not identify the open window or the incorrect parameter.

Sending the command, acknowledging receipt, applying the operating mode, and achieving the physical effect are distinct events. No response leaves the outcome unknown and requires reconciliation before retrying. The coordinator preserves the operations agenda, dependencies, and data versions; a stale result is checked before adoption. Local changes and the tasks arising from them must be saved together or recovered safely after an interruption. Confirmation of a resource does not automatically validate related commercial claims. The final explanation separately tracks what is established and what still awaits evidence.

How better patterns are built

Patterns can be designed, proposed from documents, or improved through encounters with instances. Dialogue may reveal an omitted distinction: the end of the public activity differs from the time the room is vacated after cleaning. Observed regularity does not, however, automatically become a mandatory condition. Meetings having the same duration may reflect room availability, a preference, or a policy. These explanations may coincide in known cases and require different answers in a new situation.

Learning compares candidates on contrasting cases and examples not used in their formulation. A proposal must show what it replaces and which conditions it lacks for evaluation. An adopted change produces a new pattern version, with its domain and reasons recorded. Migrating the instance may require additional information, which remains unknown when sources do not supply it. Performance on examples does not authorize changing an organization’s policy. Analogies across domains can transfer useful relationships without eliminating differences that change behavior.

The architecture’s value is tested through tasks that exercise its mechanisms: corrections, ambiguous identities, unadopted scenarios, delayed results, and recovery after interruptions. Evaluation tracks formalization, representation, revision, questions, and effects, alongside the final answer. Comparing configurations and removing components in a controlled way can reveal the contribution of justifications or links between worlds. The book’s examples are constructed for explanation, and its technological parallels are architectural interpretations. The proposal calls for demonstrating gains through observable behavior, including time, cost, and repair effort.

An initial project can begin with documents, claims, and justifications: adopt information, calculate a conclusion, receive a correction, and revise the affected consequences. A second world can use the result to prepare a publication, adding versions and approvals. The criterion remains concrete: when we return to the conversation, the system can retrieve the option discussed, explain why it seemed good, and show what has changed. This ability to continue and correct understanding is the practical promise of symbolic world models.
